ASYMPTOTICS OF a(N): HEURISTIC THEORY, NUMERICS, RIGOROUS PIECES   (experiments/asymptotics/)
=========================================================================================

Notation as in ../growth/PROOFS.txt.  S = panels bisected so far, k = |S|, f = N - k free
panels, V(S) = number of valid next panels.  "[Lean]" = Folds/Asymptotics.lean (no sorry,
no native_decide), "[paper]" = proved here, "[data]" = computed, "[heur]" = heuristic.

The imbalance field.  J(m) = J_S(m) = #(S in right arm of m) - #(S in left arm of m).
A free panel m is a valid move iff J(m) = 0, so V(S) = #{free m : J(m) = 0}.
In cyclic form J(m) = P~(2m-1) - 2 P(m-1) + (0 or -1 local term) ~ E(2c) - 2E(c),
E(x) = P(x) - k x/N the discrepancy, c = m - 1/2.

=========================================================================================
1. EXACT STRUCTURE OF THE VALIDITY CONDITION
=========================================================================================

1.1 Dipole lemma                                              [Lean: imb_cons]
-----------------
Adding a new panel x (x not in S) changes J at every panel m != x by
     +1  if  m < x <= 2m - 1        (x in the right arm of m; m in [floor(x/2)+1, x-1])
     -1  if  2m - N <= x < m        (x in the left arm of m;  m in [x+1, floor((N+x)/2)])
      0  otherwise.
The two supports have floor((x-1)/2) and floor((N-x)/2) panels (card_plus_support,
card_minus_support): every placed panel moves the field on about half of the strip
(the "1/2" of the window rule).  On the circle R/NZ the dipole of x is +1 on (x/2, x),
-1 on (x, (x+N)/2): the two preimages of x under the doubling map are its ends.
A panel whose window misses x is unaffected (imb_cons_of_not_mem_window,
semiStep_cons_iff).  smc*.c and desc.c update J by exactly this rule, O(N) per step.

1.2 Binary-digit decomposition (N = 2^L)                       [paper; dyadic.py checks it]
----------------------------------------
Write positions 0..N-1 (panel p <-> position p-1), S' = S - 1, n(w) = |S' in I_w| for the
dyadic interval I_w of a binary word w, sigma(w) = w without its first digit (sigma is the
doubling map on intervals: 2 I_{aw'} = I_{w'}).  For x = m - 1 = sum_i b_i 2^{L-i}:
     I(x) := P~(2m-1) - 2 P(m-1)
           = sum_{i=1}^{L} b_i(x) D(w_i(x))  +  1[2x mod N in S'],
     w_i(x) = b_1 .. b_{i-1} 0,     D(w) := n(sigma w) - 2 n(w)
(free m is valid iff I(x) = 0).  Proof: [0, y) is the disjoint union of the intervals
I_{b_1..b_{j-1}0} over the digits b_j(y) = 1, so P(y) = sum_{j: b_j=1} n(b_1..b_{j-1}0);
apply this to y = x and to 2x+1 mod N, whose digits are b_2..b_L 1 (the doubling map
shifts digits), and use s = n(0) + n(1) for the wrap term b_1 s.  The i-th term compares
the count in a level-(i-1) interval with twice the count in one of its two preimages under
doubling.  D(w) = 0 for all w means: the counting measure is invariant under the doubling
map, and splits equally between the two preimages.  dyadic.py verifies the identity on
every SMC snapshot (N = 256, 512, 1024; 600 sets each).
Consequence: valid sets are "doubling-balanced at every scale" along the digit path of the
new panel; the condition is a sum of L level terms, which is why discrepancies at all
scales enter and why log N (the number of scales) appears.

1.3 The acceptance is a local CLT                               [data]
---------------------------------
Along the SMC paths (uniform measure on valid prefixes) the share of free panels with J = 0 is
    frac = V/(N-k) = G / (sqrt(2 pi) sigma_J),   G = 1.02 - 1.05,
sigma_J = standard deviation of J over the free panels, for every N = 64..4096 and every
t = k/N in [1/8, 7/8] (diag3.py; the integrated mean_t log G is 0.016-0.048, table in 3.2).
The histogram of J over free panels is close to Gaussian (kurtosis 3.2-3.6, h(0) ~ h(+-1)).
So the whole question is the size of sigma_J(N, t):
    ln a(N) = ln N! - N d(N),    d(N) = mean_t ln(sqrt(2 pi) sigma_J(t)) - mean_t ln G.
(The max discrepancy Dmax = max|E| behaves like 1.6 sigma_J; growth agent's "frac x D =
0.65" is the same statement, since 1/(sqrt(2 pi) 1.6) = 0.25 ... 0.65/1.6 matches.)


=========================================================================================
2. HEURISTIC THEORY: WHY sigma_J GROWS LIKE ln N
=========================================================================================

2.1 Low discrepancy is selection, not dynamics                  [data: desc.c]
-----------------------------------------------
The uniform random greedy process (each step a uniformly random valid panel) does NOT keep
the discrepancy small: sigma_J grows like sqrt(k) (sigma_J ~ 20-70 at N = 4096, k ~ N/4),
frac drops to 0.002-0.02, and the process dies: 0 of 20 runs complete for each of
N = 64, 256, 1024, 4096, 16384 (median death at k/N = 0.35-0.6).  The uniform measure on
valid sequences is the greedy process reweighted by prod_k V(S_k):
     a(N) = E_greedy[ 1{complete} prod_k V(S_k) ]             (importance sampling identity)
          = N! E_perm[ prod_k 1{x_k valid} ] = N! E[ prod_k p_k ],  p_k ~ 1/(sqrt(2pi) sigma_J(S_k)).
So a(N) is a Feynman-Kac (directed-polymer) partition function: the path of sets S_k is
weighted by exp(-sum_k ln sigma_J(S_k)).  Low-discrepancy paths dominate the COUNT because
they have exponentially more continuations.  This is why the SMC (which resamples by V)
sees small sigma_J while unguided paths blow up, and why no "potential" is preserved by
the dynamics itself.

2.2 Fourier form of sigma_J                                      [paper, continuum version]
---------------------------
With S^(n) = sum_{x in S} e(-n x/N) and E^(n) = S^(n)/(2 pi i n),
     J^(n) = E^(n/2)[n even] - 2E^(n) = (S^(n/2)[n even] - S^(n)) / (pi i n),
     var_x J = sum_{n != 0} |Delta_n|^2 / (pi n)^2,    Delta_n := S^(n/2)[n even] - S^(n).
Every frequency n >= 1 carries one "spring" Delta_n linking n to n/2 along its doubling chain
n0, 2n0, 4n0, ... (n0 odd, pinned to 0 at the bottom).  J is small iff the Fourier transform of S
is nearly constant along doubling chains and small at odd n, with weight 1/n^2.
A random k-set has |S^(n)|^2 ~ k(1 - k/N) for all n (white), so var J ~ k/3: the Wallis model.

2.3 Gaussian Feynman-Kac model                                  [heur]
-------------------------------
Model: each step adds a point at a random position, kicking every Delta_n by an independent
unit-size random complex amount (E|kick|^2 = 2); the path weight is
     prod_k sigma_J(k)^{-1} = exp(-(1/2) sum_k ln var J(k)).
Linearise ln var J around its typical value s^2 (Hartree/self-consistent approximation):
weight ~ exp(-sum_k sum_{n>0} c_n |Delta_n(k)|^2),  c_n = 1/(pi^2 n^2 s^2).
For a random walk with diffusion D = 2 per step and a quadratic potential c|x|^2 per step,
the Feynman-Kac path measure has stationary marginal = ground state squared:
     E|Delta|^2 = 1/sqrt(2c)   (NOT the static Gibbs value 1/c),
relaxation time ~ 1/sqrt(2cD).  Hence
     E|Delta_n|^2 = pi n s / sqrt(2),    contribution to var J:  2 E|Delta_n|^2/(pi n)^2 = sqrt(2) s/(pi n),
as long as the relaxation time (~ pi n s) is below the available time ~ N, i.e. for
n < n* ~ N/(pi s); above n* the modes are free (contribution sum_{n>n*} N/(pi n)^2 ~ s/pi).
Every octave of frequencies below n* contributes the same amount sqrt(2) s ln2 / pi to var J,
so  var J = s^2 = (sqrt(2)/pi) s (ln n* + O(1)),  i.e.
     sigma_J = A (ln N - ln sigma_J) + B,      A_model = sqrt(2)/pi = 0.45.
Predictions and checks (dyadic.py, N = 256..1024, t = 1/2):
 * sigma_J grows linearly in ln N (not like sqrt(ln N), and not like a power of N).
   [The static Gibbs version of the same model gives sigma_J = O(1); the time direction is
   what makes it logarithmic: each mode is only penalised for as long as it lives.]
 * low-frequency structure factor |S^(n)|^2 ~ sqrt(2) pi s n: linear in n, prefactor ~ sigma_J,
   independent of N at fixed n.  Measured: |S^(n)|^2 = 7.7 n, 8.1 n, 8.4 n at n = 1 and
   ~8 n up to n ~ N/16 (N = 256, 512, 1024): linear in n, prefactor 0.6 x model, slowly growing.
   (A random set has |S^(n)|^2 = N/4; the valid sets are "hyperuniform" below n ~ 0.1 N.)
 * equal contribution per octave of n to var J, growing with sigma_J: measured at N = 1024,
   t = 1/2, octaves n in [2^j, 2^{j+1}): 1.72, 1.58, 1.44, 1.35, 0.99, 0.71, 0.59, 0.30, 0.16
   (model: 1.37 for n = 1, then 0.95 per octave).  At fixed octave the contribution grows
   with N (j = 2: 1.02, 1.26, 1.44 at N = 256, 512, 1024).
 * the single mode n = 1 alone contributes 1.6-1.7 to var J (model sqrt(2) s/pi = 1.37).
The model gets the mechanism and the order of magnitude; its constant A is about 30-40% low
(fitted A ~ 0.6-0.7, see 3.3).

2.4 Consequence for a(N)                                         [heur]
------------------------
With sigma_J(t) ~ A s(t) ln N (shape s(t) <= s(1/2) = 1 independent of N) and frac = G/(sqrt(2pi) sigma_J):
     ln a(N) = ln N! - N ln ln N - beta N + O(N ln ln N / ln N)
             = N ln N - N - N ln ln N - beta N + ...,
     a(N) = N! / (kappa ln N)^{N (1 + o(1))},   kappa = sqrt(2 pi) A exp(mean_t ln s(t)) / G.
So c = 1 in ln a ~ c N ln N, with the second-order term -N ln ln N (coefficient exactly 1).
Because sigma_J = A(ln N - ln sigma_J) + B, the approach is slow: the effective coefficient of
N ln ln N, fitted on N <= 2048, is 1.3-1.6, and e^{d(N)}/ln N still drifts (0.74 at N = 33,
0.92 at N = 1024, 0.96 at N = 2048) towards kappa.

4. RIGOROUS RESULTS AND THE SUPER-EXPONENTIAL LOWER BOUND
=========================================================================================
4.1 What is proved here
-----------------------
* Dipole lemma and its corollaries (1.1) [Lean].
* Binary-digit decomposition (1.2) [paper; machine-checked on 1800 sets].
* Entropy reduction [paper, standard].  For every probability distribution Q on valid
  sequences, a(N) >= exp(H(Q)) (Gibbs: log #A >= H(Q) for Q supported on A).  For a sequential
  strategy that, from every state it can reach, has a nonempty set C(S) of allowed valid
  moves (all of whose children are again such states) and picks uniformly from it,
  H(Q) = E sum_k ln |C(S_k)|.  Also, for the uniform greedy, a(N) = E[1{complete} prod_k V_k]
  exactly, so a(N) >= P(complete) exp(E[sum_k ln V_k | complete]) (Jensen).
  Hence:  a(N) >= exp(c N ln N)  follows from ANY dead-end-free strategy that has at least
  N^{c'} allowed moves on at least c''N steps.  This is the precise target; neither of the
  two ingredients (no dead ends, many moves) is hard alone - the composition skeleton has no
  dead ends and the first steps have N/2 moves - but no strategy is known that has both.

4.2 Why the natural strategies fail                              [data + paper]
-----------------------------------
* Unconstrained moves: the uniform greedy dies on 100% of runs (2.1), so P(complete) is
  exponentially small, and (2.1) shows the low-discrepancy states are reached only by
  selection.  A proof must build in the global coordination that the uniform measure gets by
  reweighting.
* Equal-count rounds = composition.  By 1.2, if every block I_u (|u| = L - r, length 2^r) holds
  the same number of points, all coarse terms D(w), |w| <= L - r, vanish (n(sigma w) = 2 n(w)
  for unions of equally filled blocks), and validity of x depends only on the fine digits:
  on the configuration inside the block of x and inside the two blocks 2u, 2u+1 (mod 2^{L-r})
  that cover its doubling image.  During a round that gives each block one new point, the
  coarse part of I(x) is I^Q_E(u) - 1[2u mod Q in E], where I^Q_E is the same function for
  the coarse strip of Q = 2^{L-r} blocks and E is the set of blocks already served in this
  round: the round is itself a Q-strip process.  If moreover every round uses one offset for
  all blocks, the rounds add whole residue classes mod 2^r and we are in the composition
  theorem a(qn) >= a(q) a(n)^q (q = 2^r offsets, n = Q blocks), which only gives
  exponential growth (PROOFS.txt 1.1).  Any super-exponential family has to let the blocks
  carry DIFFERENT configurations whose fine-scale terms still cancel - i.e. control of
  sum_i b_i D(w_i) along all digit paths at once.  This is the statistical cancellation seen
  in the data: the individual level terms b_i D(w_i) have total variance 3.8-4.5 times var I,
  the cross-level covariances cancel 74-78% of it (dyadic.py, N = 256..1024).
* Free blocks, splits, interleavings: PROOFS.txt 1.4.  The outer thirds [1, N/3] and
  [2N/3, N] are mutually non-conflicting (dipole supports), but inside each third every
  two panels conflict, so this gives one binomial factor C(2N/3, N/3), not a recursion.

4.3 Upper bounds
----------------
The local CLT frac = G/(sqrt(2pi) sigma_J) is a statement about typical states, not a bound:
crafted sets have V(S) = N - k (e.g. S = odds, where J = 0 on all evens).  A rigorous
a(N) <= N!/(something)^N would need that valid paths cannot keep many free zeros of J for
most of their length (the max path product is 0.87 ln N!, PROOFS.txt 2.4).  Nothing new is
proved here; the FK picture suggests the right object is the largest eigenvalue of the
transfer operator restricted to "low-discrepancy" states, which is not accessible.

4.4 Status of the rigorous bounds
---------------------------------
Unchanged:  (1/2) e^{1.2829 N} <= a(N) <= N! e^{-(floor((N+1)/2)-1)/16}  (PROOFS.txt).
No super-exponential lower bound was obtained.  The heuristic of section 2 says the truth is
a(N) = N! / (kappa ln N)^{N(1+o(1))}.
