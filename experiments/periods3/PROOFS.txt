PERIODS III: THE TOP COMPONENTS OF c_{k-m} WITH FREE POINTS
==========================================================
(experiments/periods3/.  Notation of ../periods/PROOFS.txt (Theorem A, face lemma, B(m)) and
../periods2/PROOFS.txt (local formula, column rule, orbit/chain vertices, Theorem C).
Throughout: m = number of "structured" points of a face, n = m - 1, D = 2^n - 1,
f = k - m = number of FREE points, c_f(N) = the coefficient of N^f in T(N, k) = T(N, m + f)
(so c_f = c_{k-m}).  h(x) = 1/(1 - e(-x)), g(x) = 1/(1 - e(x)) = 1 - h(x), e(x) = exp(2 pi i x);
h(x) = 1/2 - (i/2) cot(pi x),  g(x) = 1/2 + (i/2) cot(pi x),  |h(x)| = |g(x)| = 1/(2|sin pi x|).)

SUMMARY
  Question: is the period of c_{k-m} exactly B(m) for all k >= m?  The upper bound is Theorem A.
  For the lower bound one needs, for every prime power p^v || B(m), a nonzero Fourier component
  of c_{k-m} whose order is divisible by p^v.  Theorem C (../periods2) did this at k = m for the
  two TOP components (orders 2^n and 2^n - 1).  Here:

  1. THEOREM D1 (proved): for every m >= 4, c_1(N) of T(N, m+1) has nonzero Fourier
     components of exact orders 2^(m-1) - 1 and 2^(m-1).  (k = m + 1, one free point.)
     The proof is a closed-form computation of the one-free-point weights at the extreme
     vertices (section 2, all m), a "rotation lemma" (the rotated orbit vertices that caused
     the near-cancellation worry of ../periods2 section 6 carry NO weight for f <= 2), and a
     rigorous dominance bound (section 3).
        A_above(1/D) = (-1)^(m+1) [(m - 7) D - (m - 9)] / (4D),    A_below(1/D) = (-1)^m/(2D),
        A(1 0^(n-1)) = (-1)^(m+1) [(m-7)/4 + 3/2^m],
        A(1^n)       = (-1)^(m+1) [(m+4)/8 + e_m/2^m],   e_m = -5 (m even), +1 (m odd).
  2. THEOREM D2 (proved for 4 <= m <= 19 (odd) / 4 <= m <= 15 (2-power), and for all m given
     closed forms that are verified on 6 held-out values each): c_2(N) of T(N, m+2) has
     nonzero components of exact orders 2^(m-1) - 1 and 2^(m-1).  Exact two-free-point weight
        (-1)^(m+1) A_above(1/D) D^2 = [9 D^2 m^2 - 135 D^2 m + 22 D^2 - 6 D m^2 + 234 D m
                                        - 1012 D - 3 m^2 - 123 m + 1494] / 384,
        (-1)^(m+1) A_below(1/D) D^2 = -(D m - 6 D - m + 16)/8.
  3. STRUCTURE FOR FIXED f, m -> oo (section 6): the extreme weights are polynomials in m of
     degree f (plus O(m^f/D)), with leading coefficient vol(V_f)/f! where V_f is the set of
     valid configurations of a REDUCED problem (f points in (1/2, 1) after the limit orbit
     0, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, ...).  vol V_1 = 1/4, vol V_2 = 3/64 (matching 1/4 and 3/128 = 9/384
     above).  Since vol V_f > 0, the odd top component of c_f(T(N, m+f)) is nonzero for all
     m >= m_0(f) (Proposition E, proof outline).  The real danger is not the cotangent
     identity but SIGN CHANGES of these polynomials in m: e.g. 9m^2 - 135m + 22 changes sign
     between m = 14 and 15 (value 22 at m = 15), and the 2-power weight A1 + A3 at f = 2 nearly
     vanishes at m = 8 (0.037 against terms of size 1.25).  No exact zero occurs in any case
     computed; an exact zero would be a genuine exception to the period law.
  4. PRIME POWERS (section 8): the top components carry only a few primes of B(m): e.g. for
     m = 8, 127 and 2^7 only, while 3^2, 5, 7, 31 need cycles of length s < m - 1 (table
     primes.log).  These lower-cycle components are certified nonzero only for k <= 7
     (../prefix5); in general they remain OPEN, as does the survival of the multi-cycle
     orders 93, 105 of m = 9 in c_{k-9} for k > 9.
  5. Everything is checked against the exact Normaliz class series where available
     (k <= 7, section 7).


1. The face formula, Galois, and the dominance criterion
--------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Carriers.  By the face lemma (../periods section 3) a Fourier mode of order q in c_{k-m}
comes from codimension-m faces F with q | D_F.
  * q = D = 2^n - 1 (n != 6; for n = 6 use the factor 9 | 63): a primitive prime divisor of D
    divides 2^s - 1 only if n | s, so F has a single (U) component with s = n, e = m, a = 0: by
    Lemma 5 of ../periods this is an n-cycle of halvings in time order closed by one equality:
    the m structured points sit at an ORBIT VERTEX theta = a/D of the m-point problem
    (x_1 = theta, x_(t+1) = (x_t + c_t)/2, x_m = x_1), and the f = k - m other points are FREE.
  * q = 2^n: a single (R) component with a = n = e - 1 whose tree is a path (the denominator
    of a tree solution is 2^(depth)): a CHAIN vertex x_1 in {0,1}, x_(t+1) = (x_t + c_t)/2,
    exact denominator 2^n, plus f free points.
1.2 The face formula (../periods2 section 1, face.py; chain version chainface.py, this dir).
The coefficient of e(-N/q) in c_f(N), times f!, is
   orbit (q = D):  Phi = (1/D) sum_{a in (Z/D)^*} P(l_a) [ -A_ab(a) g(l_a/D) - A_be(a) h(l_a/D) ],
   chain (q = 2^n): Psi = (2/2^n) sum_{a odd} A(a) Pc(l_a) (-h(l_a/2^n)),
with l_a = -a^(-1) (mod D, resp. 2^n), P(l) = prod_{j<n} h(2^j l/D), Pc(l) = prod_{j<n} h(2^j l/2^n),
and the WEIGHTS
   A_ab(a), A_be(a) = sum over the C(m+f, f) time placements of the cycle among the k points of
                      the signed volume of the free configurations z in (0,1)^f for which the
                      whole configuration is valid near the face, with x_m above (resp. below)
                      x_1;  A(a) the same for the chain (x_1 = 0; the mirror x_1 = 1 gives the
                      same function of N: factor 2).
The sign of a configuration is prod over the structured columns of (+1 if the tight halving is
on its "u <= 0" side, -1 otherwise); by the column rule (../periods2 2.2) every structured
column has at most one valid side, so the sign is well defined.  Mirror vertices (theta <->
1 - theta) give identical functions of N (the reflection x -> N + 1 - x maps class to class).
Checks (this dir and ../periods2): face.py vs Normaliz at (m,k) = (4,5), (4,6), (5,6), (6,7),
(5,7), (4,7) [section 7]; chainface.py vs Normaliz at (3,4), (3,5), (4,4), (4,5), (4,6), (5,5),
(5,6), (6,6), and the k = 7 cases of section 7.  All agree to 10-12 digits.
1.3 Galois.  Phi lies in Q(zeta_D) and the coefficient of e(-N r/D) is sigma_r(Phi) (A rational,
P and g, h rational functions of zeta).  So the whole exact-order-D component vanishes iff Phi = 0;
likewise for Psi.  Moreover the l_a for a in the cyclic group <2> share the same P (the product
runs over the full doubling orbit): P(2l) = P(l).
1.4 Sizes.  Put M(l) = |P(l)|/(2|sin(pi l/D)|), Mc(l) = |Pc(l)|/(2|sin(pi l/2^n)|) (= the
modulus of one term per unit weight, since |g| = |h|), and
   R(n)  = sum_{l in (Z/D)^*, l not in +-<2>} M(l)/M(1),   Rc(n) = sum_{l odd, l != +-1} Mc(l)/Mc(1).
The weights satisfy |A_ab| + |A_be| <= W := C(m+f, f) and |A| <= W (each placement has measure
at most 1).
PROPOSITION 1.5 (dominance criterion).
 (a) Orbit.  Suppose the rotations theta = 2^i/D and 1 - 2^i/D (1 <= i <= n-1) have zero weight.
     Then Phi != 0 provided  2 cos(pi/D) |Delta_0| > W R(n),  Delta_0 := A_ab(1/D) - A_be(1/D).
 (b) Chain.  Psi != 0 provided  |A1 + (-1)^(n+1) A3| > W Rc(n),  A1 = A(1 0^(n-1)) (x_m = 2^-n),
     A3 = A(1^n) (x_m = 1 - 2^-n).
Proof.  (a) theta = 1/D has l = -1 and its mirror l = +1; their two terms are equal, so the main
term is -(2/D) P(-1) [A_ab h(1/D) + A_be g(1/D)] (g(-x) = h(x)), of modulus at least
(2/D)|P(1)| |Im(...)| = (2/D)|P(1)| |Delta_0| cot(pi/D)/2 = (2/D) M(1) cos(pi/D) |Delta_0|.
Every other a has l_a outside +-<2> (the rotations being weightless), and its term has modulus
at most (1/D) M(l_a) (|A_ab| + |A_be|) <= (W/D) M(l_a); a -> l_a is a bijection, so the rest is
at most (W/D) R(n) M(1).
 (b) With h(x) = e(x/2 - 1/4)/(2 sin pi x) and angles 2^j/2^n (j < n) plus the box angle 1/2^n
summing to 1, the unit term of 1 0^(n-1) is Z = (1/2^n) Pc(-1)(-h(-1/2^n)) = i^(n+1) Mc(1)/2^n
(checked numerically, n = 3..9), and that of 1^n is conj(Z).  So the main term is
2(A1 Z + A3 conj Z), of modulus exactly (2 Mc(1)/2^n) |A1 + (-1)^(n+1) A3| (Z real for n odd,
imaginary for n even).  The rest is at most 2 (W/2^n) Rc(n) Mc(1).  []
(For f = 0 this is Theorem C's argument: then A1 = A3 and the even-n cancellation is visible as
A1 - A3 = 0.)


2. One free point: closed forms
-------------------------------
Notation: z = the free point, inserted after p of the structured points (p = 0..m, "gap p");
R(x) = (x, (1+x)/2), L(x) = (x/2, x) the right / left arm of an earlier point x; a later point c
gets sign +1, -1, 0 from x if c is in R(x), L(x), neither; delta_t = sign of the structured
point x_t relative to z (when z is earlier, p < t).  At an orbit/chain vertex with f = 0 every
structured column t >= 2 has a fixed sum s_t from the other structured points and a forced side
(column rule).  With z earlier, the column gets s_t + delta_t; for a bit-1 column the tight
contribution is +1 (inside, sign +) or 0 (outside, sign -), for a bit-0 column 0 (sign +) or -1
(sign -).

LEMMA 2.1 (orbit 1/D, f = 1).  For every m >= 4,
     A_above(1/D) = (-1)^(m+1) [(m - 7) D - (m - 9)] / (4D),     A_below(1/D) = (-1)^m / (2D),
so  Delta_0 = (-1)^(m+1) [ (m - 7)/4 - (m - 11)/(4D) ]  (= 1/63 at m = 7, ~ (m-7)/4 in general).
Proof.  theta = 1/D, v_1 = theta, v_2 = (1 + theta)/2 = 2^(n-1)/D, v_t = 2^(m-t)/D (t = 3..m),
v_m = theta; values v_1 = v_m < v_(m-1) = 2 theta < ... < v_3 < 1/2 < v_2.  At f = 0 (../periods2
Lemma 3.1): column 2 has s = 0, columns 3..m-1 have s = +1 (from theta; v_2's left arm is
(v_3, v_2), the arms of v_3, ..., v_(t-2) miss v_t), column m has s = 0 plus the equality with
x_1.  Hence for each column t >= 2 later than z:
     delta_t = 0  -> side as at f = 0 (sign -1);     delta_t = -1 -> the other side (sign +1);
     delta_t = +1 -> invalid, except at the closing column t = m: (u <= 0, x_m BELOW x_1), sign +1.
So weight = (-1)^(m-1) (-1)^#{t > p : delta_t != 0}, above unless delta_m = +1.
The own column of z is S(z) = sum_{t <= p} sign(v_t -> z) and must vanish; for p = 0 the column
of x_1 needs sign(z -> theta) = 0.  Going through the z-intervals (all endpoints are among
v_t, v_t/2, (1+v_t)/2):
  p = 0, 1:  z in (v_2, 1) is the only valid set (z < theta, theta < z < v_2: v_3 or v_2 lies in
             R(z), delta = +1 at a halving column; p = 1, z in (theta/2, v_2): S != 0);
             delta_2 = -1 (v_2 in L(z) as z < 1 < 2 v_2), others 0: weight (-1)^m, above,
             measure 1 - v_2 = (2^(n-1) - 1)/D.
  p >= 2:  (i)   z > (1 + v_2)/2: all signs 0, no later point in an arm of z (z/2 > v_3):
                 weight (-1)^(m-1), above, measure (2^(n-1) - 1)/(2D), for every p = 2..m.
           (ii)  v_2 < z < (1 + v_2)/2: S >= 1 (v_2 and, if p >= 3, v_3 contribute +1). Invalid.
           (iii) v_3 < z < v_2: S = (+1 from theta) + (-1 from v_2) + (p - 2) (z in R(v_t), t = 3..p)
                 = p - 2: only p = 2; then delta_3 = -1: weight (-1)^m, above, measure v_3.
           (iv)  v_(j+1) < z < v_j, 3 <= j <= m-1: S = 1 for p < j, S = p - j for p >= j: only
                 p = j; then delta_(p+1) = -1 (also for p + 1 = m, the closing column, side
                 (u <= 0, above)): weight (-1)^m, above, measure v_(p+1) = 2^(m-p-1)/D.
           (v)   theta/2 < z < theta: S = -1 - [p = m] != 0.  Invalid.
           (vi)  z < theta/2: S = 0; every v_t (3 <= t <= m-1) later than z lies in R(z) (delta = +1),
                 so p >= m - 1.  p = m - 1: delta_m = +1 -> (u <= 0, BELOW), weight (-1)^m;
                 p = m: weight (-1)^(m-1), above.  Measure theta/2 = 1/(2D) each.
Summing (D A_above (-1)^m): 2(2^(n-1) - 1) + sum_{p=2}^{m-1} 2^(m-p-1) - (n - 1)(2^(n-1) - 1)/2
 - (2^(n-1) - 1)/2 - 1/2 = (2^(n-1) - 1)(7 - n)/2 - 2^(n-2) = -[(m - 7) D - (m - 9)]/4, and
D A_below = (-1)^m/2.  (probe2.py prints the intervals; probe1.py: the formula holds for m = 4..14.) []

LEMMA 2.2 (rotations, f = 1).  For m >= 6 the orbit vertices theta = 2^i/D, 1 <= i <= n-1 (and
their mirrors) admit NO valid configuration with one free point.
Proof.  The points are x_1 = 2^i/D, ..., x_(i+1) = 1/D (halvings), x_(i+2) = b := 2^(n-1)/D =
(1 + x_(i+1))/2, then halvings down to x_m = 2^i/D.
 * 2 <= i <= n - 2: column i + 2 (placing b, bit 1, tight with x_(i+1)) has the i earlier points
   x_1..x_i in R(x_s) (b < (1 + x_s)/2 since x_s > 1/D): s = i.  A single free point changes the
   sum by at most 1, and validity needs s + delta in {0, -1}: impossible for i >= 2.
 * i = n - 1: column i + 2 = m is the closing column (b = x_1), with the n - 2 points x_2..x_(n-1)
   in R: s = n - 2, and delta + (tight) + (equality) >= -1 + 0 - 1 = -2: impossible for n >= 5.
 * i = 1: column 3 has s = 1 (x_1 = 2/D) and needs delta_3 = -1, i.e. z in (b, 1) earlier than
   x_3; then column 4 (b/2, bit 0, not closing as m >= 5) has s = 2 (x_1, x_2), z contributes 0
   (b/2 < z/2), and s + (tight in {0,-1}) >= 1.  Invalid.  []
(The m = 5 exception: theta = 8/15 does carry weight, A_below = -14/15; ../periods2 section 6.)

LEMMA 2.3 (chain 1 0^(n-1), f = 1).  For m >= 4,  A1 = (-1)^(m+1) [ (m - 7)/4 + 3/2^m ].
Proof.  Same as Lemma 2.1 with theta -> 0+ (x_1 = 0 at the box), x_2 = 1/2, x_t = 2^(1-t) (t >= 2)
and NO closing equality: column m is an ordinary bit-0 column with s = +1 (from x_1), so the
f = 0 sign is (-1)^n and the rule "delta = 0: unchanged, -1: flip, +1: invalid" holds for all
columns 2..m.  Valid sets: p = 0, 1: z in (1/2, 1), weight (-1)^(n+1), measure 1/2;  p = 2..m:
z in (3/4, 1), weight (-1)^n, measure 1/4;  p = 2..m-1: z in (x_(p+1), x_p), weight (-1)^(n+1),
measure 2^(-p);  p = m: z in (x_m/2, x_m) (own column: +1 from x_1, -1 from L(x_m)), weight (-1)^n,
measure 2^(-m).  (The orbit regions (v), (vi) have measure 0 here.)  Sum: (-1)^n [-1 + (m-1)/4
- (1/2 - 2^(1-m)) + 2^(-m)].  []

LEMMA 2.4 (chain 1^n, f = 1).  For m >= 4,  A3 = (-1)^(m+1) [ (m + 4)/8 + e_m/2^m ], e_m = -5 for
m even, +1 for m odd.
Proof.  x_t = 1 - 2^(1-t) (t = 1..m), increasing; every column t >= 2 has s = 0 (the arm R(x_i)
ends at x_(i+1)), bit 1, side "outside", f = 0 sign (-1)^n; again delta = 0: unchanged, -1: flip,
+1: invalid.  For z in (x_j, x_(j+1)) (x_(m+1) := 1): R(z) contains only x_(j+1) and L(z) contains
x_2..x_j.  The own column: +1 from x_j (if j <= p), -1 from each x_t, j < t <= p (z in L(x_t),
z > 1/2); for z < 1/2, +1 from x_1 and -1 from each x_t (2 <= t <= p) with z > x_t/2 = 1/2 - 2^-t.
Valid sets:  z in (x_j, x_(j+1)) with p = j + 1 (j = 2..m-1): weight (-1)^n, measure 2^-j;
z in (1/4, 1/2) with p = 2 and z in (1/4, 3/8) with p = 3..m: weight (-1)^n, measures 1/4, 1/8;
z in (x_m, 1) with p <= m - 1: every later x_t (t >= max(p,1)+1) is in L(z): weight
(-1)^n (-1)^(m - max(p,1)), measure 2^-n; z in ((1 + x_m)/2, 1) with p = m: weight (-1)^n,
measure 2^-m.  (Everything else fails its own column or has delta = +1 at x_2 or x_(j+1).)
Sum: (-1)^n [ (1/2 - 2^-n) + 1/4 + (m-2)/8 + 2^-n (2(-1)^(m-1) + sum_{i=1}^{m-2} (-1)^i) + 2^-m ].  []
(probe6.py prints the intervals; probe5.py: Lemmas 2.3, 2.4 hold for m = 4..18.)

Consequently, in Proposition 1.5:
   orbit:  |Delta_0| = (m - 7)/4 - (m - 11)/(4D)  (m >= 7);
   chain:  |A1 + A3| = (3m - 10)/8 - 2/2^m  (m even),   |A1 - A3| = |(m - 18)/8 + 2/2^m|  (m odd).
(Note the near-cancellations: Delta_0 = 1/63 at m = 7 although the terms are O(1); |A1 - A3| =

PROPOSITION 3.1 (rigorous, all n >= 3).
  R(n)  <= Bo(n) := sum_{lam odd, 3 <= lam < 2^9} (pi/2)^(n+1) 4^b lam^-(n-b) . 2(2/lam + 2^(b+2)/D)
                    + 2^n (pi/2)^(n+1) 2^(-3n),                     (b = bit length of lam)
  Rc(n) <= Bc(n) := 2 G (pi/2) sum_{lam odd, 3 <= lam < 2^(n-1)} lam^-(n-b+1),
                    G = prod_{k>=1} (pi/2^k)/sin(pi/2^k) = 1.8090...
  Values: Bo(26) = 1.5e-5, Bo(30) = 1.1e-6, Bo(40) = 1.7e-9;  Bc(10) = 5.9e-3, Bc(26) = 1.7e-7;
  both decrease in n (every term does).
Proof (orbit).  For l in (Z/D)^* let d_j = min(c_j, D - c_j), c_j = 2^j l mod D, and N(O) =
prod_j d_j (depends only on the orbit pair O u -O).  sin(pi t) <= pi t and sin(pi t) >= 2t
(0 <= t <= 1/2) give M(l)/M(1) <= (pi/2)^(n+1) N(1)/(N(O) d_0(l)), with
N(1) = 2^((n-1)(n-2)/2) (2^(n-1) - 1) < 2^(n(n-1)/2).  Let lam = min of the d_j over O u -O.
lam is odd (else lam/2 = 2^-1 lam is smaller), and the binary word of lam/D is the n-bit
expansion of lam: n - b zeros, then the b bits of lam (first and last = 1).  For j < n - b,
d_j = 2^j lam (< D/2); for the b shifts starting inside lam's bits the leading run has length
<= b - i, and a binary expansion starting with a run of length r has distance >= 2^-(r+1) to Z,
so d_j >= D 2^-(b-i+1).  Hence N(O) >= lam^(n-b) 2^((n-b)(n-b-1)/2) D^b 2^-(b(b+3)/2), and with
D >= 2^(n-1) a short computation gives N(1)/N(O) < 4^b lam^-(n-b).  Summing 1/d_0(l) over the
<= 2n elements of O u -O gives <= 2(2/lam + 2^(b+2)/D).  For b >= 10 use instead: lam minimal
forces every run of the cyclic word to have length <= n - b, so log2 N(O) >= n log2 D -
sum_runs r(r+3)/2 >= n(n-1) - n(n-b)/2 - 3n/2, i.e. N(1)/N(O) < 2^(n(4-b)/2) <= 2^-3n; there are
fewer than 2^n such l and d_0 >= 1.
(chain).  t_i(l) = ||l/2^i|| (i = 1..n; the n factors and the box factor use t_n).  For lam odd,
t_i(lam) >= 2^-i = t_i(1), and 1/(2 sin pi t) decreases on (0, 1/2]: the factors with i <= b are
at most those of l = 1.  For i >= b + 1, t_i(lam) = lam/2^i and
sin(pi/2^i)/sin(pi lam/2^i) <= gamma(lam/2^i)/lam, gamma(y) = pi y/sin(pi y) increasing; the
product of the gamma's over i >= b+1 is at most G (lam/2^(b+1) < 1/2), and the box factor
contributes <= (pi/2)/lam.  So Mc(+-lam)/Mc(1) <= G (pi/2) lam^-(n-b+1).  []


4. Theorem D1
-------------
THEOREM D1.  For every m >= 4 the coefficient c_1(N) of N in T(N, m+1) has a nonzero Fourier
component of exact order 2^(m-1) - 1 and a nonzero component of exact order 2^(m-1).
Proof.  Odd part, m >= 8: Lemmas 2.1, 2.2 and Proposition 1.5(a) with W = m + 1: the inequality
2 cos(pi/D)|Delta_0| > (m+1) R(m-1) holds for 8 <= m <= 25 with the numerical R (margin >= 5.8x,
thmD.log) and for m >= 26 with Bo (LHS >= 9.5, RHS <= 7.7e-4 and decreasing).  m = 4..7: the full
face sums (all orbit vertices, exact weights, face.py; ../periods2/face_table.log):
     m = 4: -0.054279 + 0.042425i   m = 5: -0.093333 - 0.014297i   m = 6: 0.362440 + 0.048070i

6. Fixed f, m -> infinity: the reduced problem
----------------------------------------------
Why the weights are polynomials in m.  Fix f and consider theta = 1/D (or the chain 1 0^(n-1)).
Its structured points are theta ~ 0, v_2 ~ 1/2, v_3 ~ 1/4, ..., a doubling-scale cascade towards 0,
of length m.  A free point z is "time-free" if its validity and its effect do not depend on
where in the long cascade it is inserted: this happens exactly for z > 1/2 inserted after the
first few structured points (z is then in no arm of the tiny v_t, and no tiny v_t is in an arm of
z; z interacts only with v_2, ..., v_j where v_j > 2z - 1).  Each time-free free point has
~m admissible gaps; every other free point has O(1) admissible gaps (Lemma 2.1 (iii)-(vi): a
point inside the cascade is tied to its own scale, a point below theta needs gap >= m - O(f)),
or lies in a set of measure O(1/D).  Time-free configurations change no structured column
(no flips), so they all carry the sign (-1)^(m+1) and are "above".  This gives:
PROPOSITION E (outline; constants not made explicit).  For fixed f,
   Delta_0(m, f) = (-1)^(m+1) [ vol(V_f)/f! . m^f + O_f(m^(f-1)) ],
where V_f = { (z_1..z_f) in (1/2,1)^f (time order) : for every i,
                c(z_i) + sum_{j<i} sign(z_j -> z_i) = 0 },   c(z) = floor(log2 1/(2z - 1)),
is the valid set of the REDUCED problem: f points placed after the limit orbit 0, 1/2, 1/4, ...
(c(z) = number of limit points 2^-j with z in R(2^-j)).  V_f has nonempty interior (e.g.
z_1 > 3/4, z_(i+1) > (1 + z_i)/2), so vol V_f > 0.  Together with the rotation argument (for
n > f + 4 only the rotations i <= f can carry weight; their cotangent factors are O(2^f) instead of
cot(pi/D) ~ D/pi) and R(n) -> 0, Proposition 1.5(a) gives: the order-(2^(m-1) - 1) component of
c_f(T(N, m+f)) is nonzero for all m >= m_0(f).
Checks:  vol V_1 = 1/4 (V_1 = (3/4, 1)): leading coefficient 1/4 of Lemma 2.1.
         vol V_2 = 1/64 (z_2 > 3/4: z_2 > (1+z_1)/2) + 1/32 (z_2 in (5/8, 3/4): c = 1, z_2 in L(z_1))
                 = 3/64: leading coefficient 3/128 = 9/384 of the f = 2 closed form.
For the increasing chain 1^n the reduced problem lives in (0, 1/2) with field
c3(z) = 1 - #{t >= 2 : z > 1/2 - 2^-t}: vol V_1^(3) = 1/8 (Lemma 2.4: (m+4)/8), vol V_2^(3) = 3/256
+ 1/128 = 5/256.  So for the 2-part the leading coefficient of A1 - A3 (n even) is
(vol V_f - vol V_f^(3))/f!: 1/8 at f = 1, 7/512 at f = 2 -- nonzero, but not obviously for all f.
Monte Carlo (reduced_mc.py, 2.10^6 samples; reduced_mc.log):
     f              1          2          3          4
   vol V_f        0.24994    0.046857   0.008851   0.001474     (exact 1/4, 3/64 = 0.046875)
   vol V_f^(3)    0.12485    0.019547   0.002709   0.000290     (exact 1/8, 5/256 = 0.019531)
so the predicted leading coefficients at f = 3 are 0.001475 m^3 (odd part, A_above(1/D)) and
(0.001475 -+ 0.000451) m^3 for A1 -+ A3: all nonzero.
WHAT FAILS for a proof for all k >= m: (i) uniformity in f (m_0(f) grows; the fixed-m, k -> oo
regime is a continuum problem as hard as c_k itself, cf. ../periods section 6); (ii) the
polynomials in m can change sign (f = 2: between m = 14 and 15), and an integer zero of the
leading part would leave only O(m^f/D)-size terms -- the mechanism by which an exception to the
period law could occur.  No zero has been found.

8. The other prime powers of B(m); multi-cycle components
---------------------------------------------------------
8.1 Reduction.  period(c_{k-m}) = B(m) iff for every prime power p^v || B(m) some nonzero Fourier
component of c_{k-m} has order divisible by p^v (the period is the lcm of the orders of the
nonzero exact-order components).  The 2-part 2^(m-1): order-2^(m-1) component (Theorem C at
f = 0, D1 at f = 1, D2 at f = 2).  An odd prime p has v_p(B(m)) = max_{s<=m-1} v_p(2^s - 1) =
v_p(2^o - 1) + J, o = ord_p(2), J = max{j : o p^j <= m-1} (lifting the exponent).  The top odd
component 2^(m-1) - 1 carries the full p-part iff o p^J | m - 1.  primes.py lists the rest:
   m = 4: 3 (needs s = 2);  m = 5: 7 (s = 3);  m = 6: 3, 5 (s = 4), 7 (s = 3);
   m = 7: 5 (s = 4), 31 (s = 5);  m = 8: 3^2, 7 (s = 6), 5 (s = 4), 31 (s = 5);  ...
   m = 14: everything except 8191.
So for most m almost all odd primes of B(m) need components carried by a CYCLE OF LENGTH
s < m - 1 together with m - 1 - s further equations (a pendant halving tree on the cycle, giving
the order 2^(m-1-s)(2^s - 1), or a box chain / marks, giving 2^a'(2^s - 1) with a' < m - 1 - s).
Theorems C, D1, D2 do not touch these.  They are certified nonzero for k <= 7 (../prefix5: every
divisor of every 2^a(2^s - 1), a + s = m - 1, occurs as an exact order in c_{k-m}).
8.2 The vertex level for the mixed orders (vertsq.py, nonsimple.py).  An order q is NEW at m if
no face of codimension < m has an index divisible by q; for such q the exact-order-q part of
c_0(T(N, m)) is the sum of the vertex formula over the distinct vertices with q | index (only
characters with all w_r != 0 carry exact order q).  The orders 2^a (2^s - 1), a + s = m - 1, are
new at m.  vertsq.py enumerates all vertices of all class polytopes (Normaliz extreme rays,
../periods/types_K.txt) and sums the vertex formula.  NEW INGREDIENT: the natural "cycle + pendant
chain" vertices (theta, ..., theta, theta/2, theta/4, ...) are NOT SIMPLE: x_(s+2) = theta/2 is a
halving of both copies of theta, so k + 1 rows are tight with one dependency u_a - u_b = u_e.
nonsimple.py handles them: each class's tangent cone is one consistent sign pattern, which is an
orthant in the basis obtained by dropping the row whose sign the others imply; the lattice points
then satisfy the dropped row with its strictness automatically.
