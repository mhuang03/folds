SEARCH FOR A RECURSIVE FORMULA a(N) = F(a(1), ..., a(N-1), N)        (experiments/recsearch/)
=========================================================================================

Question: is there a recurrence for the semi-symmetric fold counts a(N) that uses only
earlier terms of the sequence (and N)?  Data: a(1..37) (experiments/semi_counts.txt), and
c(1..41), the reversal-mirror-fixed counts (experiments/arith/selfdual_out.txt).  All
arithmetic is exact (Python integers/Fractions, python-flint for exact kernels and LLL).
At most 1 core and < 2 GB were used.

BOTTOM LINE
-----------
Nothing works.  None of about 5,600 linear ansatze (P-recursive in 20 transforms,
divide-and-conquer, convolution, divisor-sum, algebraic and differentially-algebraic GFs)
and none of ~10^6 expression trees (searched pairwise, ~10^12 combinations) reproduce the
held-out terms.  There is not even a near miss: the largest "excess" is +2 (a fit that
matches 2 more of the first equations than its free parameters allow), and every such
case comes from the tiny initial terms.  The best expression tree matches N = 3..6 and
then fails.
  * PROVEN (unconditional): the OGF sum a(N) x^N is not algebraic, because
    a(N) >= (N/512)^{N/22} grows faster than any exponential.
  * PROVEN only conditionally: a(N) is not P-recursive (holonomic) if the asymptotic
    conjecture holds.  The proven bounds alone do NOT exclude P-recursiveness (section T).
  * EMPIRICAL (exact linear algebra): no P-recursive relation of order r and degree d with
    (r+1)(d+1) <= 32 - r, i.e. up to 30 unknowns with >= 5 spare equations.  This uses all
    37 terms and holds for each of the 20 sequences/transforms listed in section 1.
    The lattice search adds: for every box with 32 - r < (r+1)(d+1) <= 64 there is no
    relation whose coefficient vector has small norm (bounds in section 1b).
  * a(N) mod p: for every prime 3 <= p <= 97 there is no linear recurrence over F_p of
    order <= 16 valid for N = 1..37 (order <= 18 for p >= 5).  Even from N = 8 on there
    is none of order <= 13.  a(N) is not ultimately periodic mod m for any 3 <= m <= 64.
    The only modular structure is the known even-ness and a == c (mod 4).

Notation for the table: K = number of unknown coefficients, E = number of exact equations,
spare = E - K.  For linear ansatze the "fit / held-out" split is automatic: a fit must
satisfy all E equations, and K of them determine it (the first K equations are the fit,
the remaining E-K >= 5 are held out).  nfit = largest prefix of equations that still has
a solution with non-zero target coefficient.  The generic value is Keff-1, and
excess = nfit-(Keff-1).  Rank is certified mod two 61-bit primes (full rank mod p implies
full rank over Q); every deficient case is recomputed over Q with flint.  Sanity check
(selftest.py): the engine finds n! = n(n-1)!, the Motzkin recurrence, and flags a Motzkin
sequence corrupted after n = 20 as a near miss (excess +12).

1b. LATTICE SEARCH BEYOND THE EXACT RANGE  (lll.py, lll_out.txt, lll_compare_out.txt)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For 32-r < K = (r+1)(d+1) <= 64 (r = 1..12) there are fewer equations than unknowns,
so rational relations always exist.  The test is whether one has a SMALL integer
coefficient vector.  Embed: lattice of all (c, W*M c), with W = 10^60.  By the LLL
guarantee |b1| <= 2^((K-1)/2) lambda_1, every relation c has
   |c| >= B = |b1|/2^((K-1)/2)  when b1 is itself a relation,  and
   |c| >= W/2^((K-1)/2) ~ 10^54  otherwise.
This is a rigorous lower bound on the coefficient size of any recurrence in that box.
  * Positive control (planted order-4, degree-3 recurrence with coefficients in [-3,3]
    and n!-type growth): found exactly in every box containing it (K = 20..40),
    |b1| ~ 10^0.9.
  * a(N): no relation of norm < 10^54 for K <= 32-ish (where b1 is not a relation).  In
    the larger boxes the shortest relations are huge: log10|b1| falls from ~54 (K ~ 33)
    to 12.1 at K = 60 (r = 11, d = 4).  This is the generic decline as the kernel
    dimension grows.  The lower bounds B are listed per box in lll_out.txt; the smallest
    is 10^3.2 (r=11, d=4, K=60), and every box with K <= 48 has B >= 10^9.
  * Size-matched negative controls (a(N) multiplied by random factors in [0.9,1.1] or
    [0.99,1.01] for N >= 8, which surely have no relation): over the 51 boxes, a(N)'s
    |b1| is smaller by a mean of 1.04 orders of magnitude (range 0.2-3.4) than the 10%
    control.  Against the 1% control the gap is a mean of 0.68 orders (range 0.3-1.8).
    The gap shrinks as the noise shrinks, so it reflects the smoothness of a(N) (its
    log has residuals ~0.03 around a smooth trend), not a hidden relation.  A real
    relation shows up as a drop of ~50 orders, as in the positive control.
    (lll_compare_out.txt has the box-by-box comparison.)

T. THEORY: GROWTH VERSUS D-FINITENESS / ALGEBRAICITY
------------------------------------------------------
Proven facts used (README, experiments/growth/PROOFS.txt, Lean):
   (N/512)^{N/22} <= a(N) <= N! exp(-(floor((N+1)/2)-1)/16),
   liminf ln a/(N ln N) >= 1/22, limsup ln a/(N ln N) >= 1/6.
Conjecture: a(N) = N!/(kappa_N ln N)^N, i.e. ln a(N) = N ln N - N ln ln N + O(N).

(T1) The OGF is not algebraic, not rational and not C-finite.  PROVEN.  Algebraic power
     series have coefficients O(rho^N).  a(N) >= (N/512)^{N/22} is super-exponential,
     so sum a(N) x^N has radius of convergence 0.
(T2) Classical fact (Perron's theorem for limsup, extended to Newton polygons by Perron
     and Kreuser).  This is the form used, e.g., by Bell, Gerhold, Klazar and Luca,
     "Non-holonomicity of sequences defined via elementary functions", Ann. Comb. 2008.
     If a_n satisfies a linear recurrence with polynomial coefficients and is not
     eventually 0, then there are a rational slope kappa of the recurrence's Newton
     polygon and a non-zero algebraic lambda with
         limsup_n | a_n / (n!)^kappa |^{1/n} = |lambda|,   0 < |lambda| < infinity.
     In particular ln a_n <= kappa n ln n + O(n), with equality up to O(n) infinitely
     often, so limsup ln a_n/(n ln n) = kappa is RATIONAL and the limsup of
     (a_n/n!^kappa)^{1/n} is finite and NON-ZERO.
(T3) Conditional non-holonomicity.  Assume the conjecture, or just
         (i) ln a(N)/(N ln N) -> 1   and   (ii) (a(N)/N!)^{1/N} -> 0.
     By (i) and T2, kappa = 1.  Then T2 forces limsup (a(N)/N!)^{1/N} = |lambda| > 0,
     which contradicts (ii).  So a(N) is NOT P-recursive, the OGF is not D-finite, and
     the EGF is not D-finite.  Equivalently: under the conjecture the EGF is entire of
     infinite order, since a(N)/N! = (kappa ln N)^{-N}; D-finite entire functions have
     finite order.  The conjecture's (ln N)^{-N} factor is exactly the kind of growth no
     holonomic sequence can have, because holonomic growth is
     n!^{p/q} rho^n exp(poly(n^{1/s})) n^alpha (ln n)^beta, with beta fixed.
(T4) The PROVEN bounds alone do NOT rule out P-recursiveness.  The bounds only give
     limsup ln a/(N ln N) in [1/6, 1] and (a/N!)^{1/N} <= e^{-1/32+o(1)}.  Both are
     compatible with T2, for example with kappa = 1 and |lambda| <= e^{-1/32}, or with
     any rational kappa in [1/6, 1).  Concretely, the P-recursive sequence
     b(N) = N!/2^{N/2} (adjust to an integer, e.g. (2n)!/2^n at N = 2n) satisfies the
     upper bound, the lower bound and the structural inequalities:
       b(2n) >= 2 b(n)^2, because C(2n,n) >= 2;
       b(qn) >= b(q) b(n)^q, because (qn)!/(n!)^q >= q!.
     So none of the proven inequalities can separate a from holonomic sequences.  The
     mod-4 congruence a == c and the mod-3 growth effect do not either: P-recursive
     sequences can have any finite residue pattern and periodic fluctuations.  What is
     missing for a proof is an UPPER bound of the form a(N) <= N! e^{-omega(N)}, i.e.
     (a(N)/N!)^{1/N} -> 0, together with ln a/(N ln N) -> 1.  Alternatively, it would be
     enough to show that limsup ln a/(N ln N) is irrational or that (a/N!^kappa)^{1/N}
     tends to 0 or infinity for the right kappa.  Proving any of these is a growth
     question, not a recurrence question.
(T5) D-algebraic (algebraic ODE) GFs have no comparable growth obstruction: Maillet-type
     bounds allow n!^c growth.  So family 4' is purely empirical.
(T6) Related, already in the repo: the bivariate prefix GF sum T(N,k) t^N u^k is proven
     non-D-finite (experiments/periods2/PROOFS.txt).  This is evidence that the counting
     problem has no finite transfer structure, but it does not imply anything about
     a(N) itself.

NEAR MISSES (honest list)
--------------------------
* Linear families: the largest genuine excess anywhere is +2 (conv family, parity
  basis).  It comes from N <= 5, where a(N) = 1, 2, 4, 10, 28 satisfy many small
  identities, e.g. a(N) = 3a(N-1) - 2 for N = 3, 4, 5.  No fit survives past N = 6.
* Expression trees: the best single tree, (1/3)(a(N-1)+a(N-3))(n+N), matches N = 3..6
  and fails at N = 7.  No tree in any of the six forms matches N = 3..20 (the fit range).
* The excess in prefix fits is 0 or 1 for nearly all of the ~5,600 linear ansatze.  For
  comparison, the corrupted Motzkin sequence gives +12.

WHAT THIS MEANS
---------------
The only exact recursion known stays the subset recursion of the README
(a(N) = sum_{|S|=k} g(S) g(S^c)).  It recurses over 2^N subsets, not over earlier terms
of the sequence.  The lower-bound constructions (parity blocks, composition, doubling
splits) give inequalities only: their share of a(N) tends to 0 (e.g. the parity
construction's share is 0.0016 at N = 20), so they cannot be the leading terms of an
exact recurrence.  This explains why every family of type 2 fails.  A recurrence in
the earlier terms alone would have to be of a type outside everything tested here.
Under the growth conjecture it cannot be linear with polynomial coefficients of any
order or degree (T3).
