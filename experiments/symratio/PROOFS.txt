THE RATIO theta(n) / (2 theta(floor n/2) theta(ceil n/2)): WINDOWS, AN EXACT RECURSION,
AND CONJECTURE R                                                       (experiments/symratio/)
==========================================================================================
Builds on ../symstruct/PROOFS.txt (Theorem M, the identity theta(n) = sum P(sigma,tau),
Conjecture R) and ../symrun/PROOFS.txt (opening runs).  theta(n) = A003407(n) = number of
3-AP-free permutations of [1,n].  Lean: Folds/SymRatio.lean (no sorry, no native_decide;
axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound).

Notation.  N >= 2, p in {0,1}; D = values of parity p in [1,N] (d = floor((N+p)/2) of them),
C = the others (c = floor((N+1-p)/2)); lift_p(r) = 2r - p maps [1,d] onto D.  For sigma in
Theta(d), tau in Theta(c), P(sigma,tau) = number of 3-AP-free permutations of [1,N] starting
in D whose D-subsequence is lift(sigma) and whose C-subsequence is lift(tau) (Lean
SymStructure2.shuffles).  theta(N) = sum_p sum_{sigma,tau} P(sigma,tau) (Theorem M), so
r(N) := theta(N)/(2 theta(floor N/2) theta(ceil N/2)) = (E_0 + E_1)/2, E_p = mean of P over
independent uniform sigma, tau.
Windows: head(a) = first 6 entries, tail(a) = last 6 entries (as lists).

SUMMARY
  W  (locality) P(sigma,tau) = Pi_{N,p}(tail sigma, head tau): it depends only on the last 6
     entries of sigma and the first 6 of tau.  Pi counts the interleavings of the two
     lifted 6-windows with the D-entry first on every linked pair.             [Lean, N >= 14]
  S  r(N) = (1/2) sum_p sum_{S,T} t_d(S) h_c(T) Pi_{N,p}(S,T), t_m / h_m = laws of the tail /
     head window of a uniform element of Theta(m) (h_m = t_m reversed): r(N) is a fixed
     bilinear form in the boundary statistics at sizes floor(N/2), ceil(N/2).   [Lean]
  J  the joint head/tail law J_m satisfies an EXACT closed recursion under halving
     (m >= 19; Lean m >= 24).  Consequence: theta(n) in O(n^3) time and O(n^2) memory.
     Checked: equals the b-file for all 19 <= n <= 200; new values theta(201..300).
  Q  exact algebra: the head-tail correlation of J_m is QUADRATIC in those of the halves
     (an identity for J - h x t).
  N  numerics: r(n) for all n <= 520 and along doubling chains to n ~ 1800; the doubling
     defect contracts by a factor -> 4.00 per doubling for x not divisible by 3 and
     super-geometrically for 3 | x (a new dichotomy).  Conjecture R: still open.

1. THEOREM W (the window / locality theorem)          [Lean SymRatio.card_shuffles_eq_win]
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEMMA 1.1 (sharp opening run, Lean run_prefix / run_take).  Let a be a 3-AP-free
permutation of [1,N] whose first entry has parity q, K = floor((N+q)/2) (the size of its
class).  Then the first K - 6 entries of a have parity q.
Proof.  If a[k] has the other parity and k < K - 6, at most k entries of parity q precede
it, so at least K - k > 6 follow it, contradicting Sharma's ends theorem in the form
SymStructure2.D_after_le_six (at most 6 entries of the first parity follow any entry of
the other parity).                                                                    QED
LEMMA 1.2 (closing run).  The last c - 6 entries have parity 1 - q (c = size of the other
class).  Proof: reverse(a) is 3-AP-free and starts with the last entry of a, which has
parity 1 - q (first and last entries differ in parity, SymParity.head_last_parity);
apply 1.1.                                                                            QED
THEOREM W.  Let d >= 7, c >= 6 (true for both p when N >= 14), sigma in Theta(d), tau in
Theta(c), k = d - 6, S = sigma.drop k (last 6), T = tau.take 6 (first 6), X = lift_p of
sigma.take k, Y = lift_{1-p} of tau.drop 6.  Then
   a  |->  w = the 12 entries of a after position k
is a bijection from the shuffles of (sigma,tau) onto
   Win_{N,p}(S,T) = { interleavings w of lift_p(S) and lift_{1-p}(T) (each in its order)
                      such that y precedes u for every linked pair y in D, u in C },
with inverse w |-> X ++ w ++ Y.  Hence P(sigma,tau) = Pi_{N,p}(S,T) := |Win_{N,p}(S,T)|.
Proof.  (a = X ++ w ++ Y.)  By 1.1 the first k entries of a are D-entries; the D-subsequence
of a is lift(sigma), so they are X.  By 1.2 the last c - 6 entries are C-entries, hence
equal to Y (Lean: through the reversal, filter_take_of_forall).  The middle w has
D-subsequence lift(S) and C-subsequence lift(T) (cancel X, Y in the filters), so it is an
interleaving; inside a contiguous block the order is that of a, so linked pairs in w are
in D-before-C order (Lean bef_of_infix).  (X ++ w ++ Y is a shuffle.)  Its D- and
C-subsequences are lift(sigma), lift(tau), so it is a permutation of [1,N] starting with
X_1 in D (Lean lift_union_isPermOf).  For a linked pair (y,u): y in X precedes everything
after X; y in w and u in Y: y first; y, u in w: by the hypothesis on w; y in Y or u in X
is impossible (parities).  By Theorem M (Lean shuffle_key, inside mem_shuffles) it is
3-AP-free.  The two maps are mutually inverse (|X| = k, |w| = 12).                     QED
Explicit form.  With y_1..y_6 = lift(S), u_1..u_6 = lift(T) and c_j = number of y's before
u_j:   Pi = #{ 0 <= c_1 <= ... <= c_6 <= 6 : c_j >= M_j },
       M_j = max_{i <= j} max{ k : Linked(N, y_k, u_i) }  (0 if none).
So Pi <= C(12,6) = 924, and Pi = 1 iff M_1 = 6, i.e. u_1 is linked to y_6 (the last entry
of sigma).  Since c_1 >= d - 6 is forced in the full shuffle, every pair occurring has
M_1 >= 1 [Sharma].  Lean corollary card_shuffles_congr: equal windows => equal P.
The window is optimal in general: 6 = Sharma's constant is sharp (../symrun, f(48*2^j+2)).

2. THEOREM S (r(N) is a bilinear form in boundary statistics) [Lean card_symSeqs_eq_stat_sum]
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For N >= 14,
   theta(N) = sum_p sum_S sum_T alpha_p(S) beta_p(T) Pi_{N,p}(S,T),
   alpha_p(S) = #{sigma in Theta(d) : tail sigma = S},  beta_p(T) = #{tau in Theta(c) : head = T}.
Dividing by theta(d) theta(c):  r(N) = (1/2) sum_p <t_d, Pi_{N,p} h_c>.  By reversal
h_m(T) = t_m(reverse T), so r(N) is determined by the two tail laws t_{floor N/2},
t_{ceil N/2}.  This is Goal 2(a), with "boundary statistics" = the law of the 6-window.
Their support is finite for each m but grows linearly: the head windows are exactly the
live 6-prefixes of ../symstruct Theorem L, L_6(m) ~ (95/4) m; measured 24.2 m (nh column).
Why not fewer statistics: Pi depends on the exact linkage pattern of 6 x 6 values, i.e. on
comparisons like 2y <= N + u at resolution 1; no finite quotient of the windows is
invariant under all N (the scaled positions y/N matter), see section 5.

3. THEOREM J (exact propagation of the window statistics)  [Lean card_window_eq_sum, N >= 24]
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
J_m(H,T) = #{a in Theta(m) : head a = H, tail a = T}.  For m >= 19,
   J_m(lift_p H', lift_{1-p} T') = sum_{S,T''} J_d(H',S) Pi_{m,p}(S,T'') J_c(T'',T'),
and J_m vanishes off these (p = parity of the first entry).
Proof.  By Theorem W, a shuffle is X ++ w ++ Y.  Its head is lift(head sigma) when its
opening run has length >= 6, and its tail is lift(tail tau) when its closing run (= the
opening run of the reversal) has length >= 6.  For m >= 24 both hold because d, c >= 12
(Lemma 1.1/1.2; Lean shuffle_head, shuffle_tail).  For 19 <= m <= 23 use the exact minimal
run f(m) = 6,6,6,6,7 (../symstruct section 3, table; f(18) = 5, so 19 is optimal).  Sum
the window identity over the shuffles with the prescribed windows (Lean
card_filter_symSeqs_eq_sum refines card_symSeqs_eq_sum by any predicate).          QED
ALGORITHM (window.c).  Enumerate Theta(m) for 9 <= m <= 18 (the base, theta(18) = 659296),
then apply Theorem J upward; theta(m) = sum of J_m.  Blocks: J_m splits by the parity p of
the first entry, J_{m,p} = blockdiag(J_d) Pi_{m,p} blockdiag(J_c) (dense matrix products of
size ~ 12m).  For theta(n) one needs J only at the O(log n) sizes floor/ceil(n/2^i),
i >= 2, the marginals t, h at n/2, and theta(n) = sum_p <t_d, Pi h_c>:
     time O(n^3), memory O(n^2)    (window lists have length L_6(m) = O(m)).
So A003407 is computable in polynomial time.  (The methods in docs/LITERATURE.md are
memoised DP over subsets, Correll-Ho, n <= 90; the b-file stops at n = 200.  We have not
checked whether Ho's 2026 code uses a similar decomposition.)
VERIFICATION.  window_mod.bin (exact, Montgomery arithmetic mod a prime) reproduces the
b-file modulo 2^61 - 1 for every 19 <= n <= 200 (checkmod.py: 182 values, 0 mismatches);
the double-precision version gives r(n) with max error 4.9e-13 against the b-file.
Cost: all n <= 200 in 33 s (exact) / 22 s (double); all n <= 520 in ~7 min, 1.3 GB.
NEW VALUES theta(201..300): data/theta_exact.txt (six primes below 2^62 + CRT, crt.py;
each value checked to have the size predicted by the double run, and the same pipeline
reproduces the b-file for n <= 200).  E.g.

4. THEOREM Q (head-tail decoupling is quadratic)                          [paper; algebra]
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a nonnegative matrix M of total mass 1 write m_r = M 1, m_c = 1^T M and
M' = M - m_r m_c^T (so M' 1 = 0, 1^T M' = 0), kappa(M) = ||M'||_1.
IDENTITY.  Let A (heads x S) and B (T x tails) have mass 1, Pi >= 0, J = A Pi B and
X = 1^T J 1 = alpha^T Pi b (alpha = 1^T A, b = B 1).  With u = A' Pi b, v = alpha^T Pi B',
       J - (J 1)(1^T J)/X  =  A' Pi B'  -  u v^T / X.
Proof.  A = a alpha^T + A', B = b beta^T + B'.  Expanding, J = X a beta^T + a v^T + u beta^T
+ A' Pi B', J 1 = X a + u, 1^T J = X beta^T + v^T (cross terms vanish by A'1 = 0, 1^T A' = 0,
B'1 = 0), and (X a + u)(X beta + v)^T / X = X a beta^T + a v^T + u beta^T + u v^T / X.  QED
COROLLARY.  kappa(J/X) <= (pi/X)(1 + pi/X) kappa(A) kappa(B), pi = max Pi.
Applied to Theorem J with A = J_d, B = J_c (one value of p): the head-tail correlation of a
random element of Theta(m) inside the block p is QUADRATIC in the correlations of the two
halves.  (The head of a is the lifted head of sigma, the tail the lifted tail of tau, and
sigma, tau are coupled only through Pi(tail sigma, head tau).)
What it does not give.  (i) The constant (pi/X)(1+pi/X) is ~ 250 (pi = 20, X ~ 1.3).
(ii) The parity blocks: J_m is a mixture over p of blocks with disjoint supports (the
parity of the first entry fixes that of the last), a correlation of order 1 outside the
identity; inside a block the same happens one binary digit deeper, and so on.  So J_m is,
up to quadratic errors, a mixture over "parity paths" of products of head and tail laws.
A proof of Conjecture R would have to control this path mixture.

5.3 THE MOD-3 DICHOTOMY (new).  Along a chain x 2^j (x odd):
  * 3 does not divide x: r(x 2^{j+1}) - r(x 2^j) ~ A(x) 4^{-j}, the ratio of consecutive
    differences tends to 4.0 (x = 5: 5.25e-3, 1.58e-3, 3.98e-4, 9.73e-5; x = 7, 11, 13, ...
    alike).  Richardson: Phi(x) ~ r(2n) + (r(2n) - r(n))/3.
  * 3 divides x: after a transient the differences collapse much faster than any fixed
    geometric rate: x = 3: 1.40e-3 (24->48), -3.26e-6, -7.36e-10 (96->192, exact rational
    from the b-file), < 1e-12 (192->384); x = 9: r(72) = 1.316957343, r(144) = 1.316957146,

  The window statistics show the same split (stats_130.txt): maxPi = 6 at m = 2^k, 10 when
  3 | m, 20 (or 15) otherwise; Pr(Pi = 1) is identical at 48 and 96 (0.788826).
5.4 Head-tail correlation (stats mode: kappa(m) = total variation between J_m and the
  product of its marginals, inside each first-entry parity block).  It decays like 4^{-j}
  along chains with 3 not dividing x (2.63e-3 at 32, 6.48e-4 at 64, 1.58e-4 at 128) and
  super-geometrically for 3 | m (3.4e-6 at 48, 5.9e-10 at 96; 2.9e-7 at 72).  For
  m = 0 mod 4 (x even): |r(2m) - r(m)| = (0.62..0.68) kappa(m) for every 32 <= m <= 130.
  So the doubling defect of r IS the head-tail correlation at half size, as the window
  picture predicts, and Theorem Q explains why that correlation is small.
5.5 Ties.  Pr(some 3-AP through a window pair ends at 0 or n+1) (the only pairs whose
  linkage changes under lifting) decays only like 2^{-j} (0.82, 0.49, 0.26 at 24, 48, 96):
  ties are not rare, their effect on r is.

PROVED (paper only): Theorem J for 19 <= m <= 23 (needs the f-table), Theorem Q, the
complexity bound, the correctness of window.c (it implements Theorem J literally; also
validated against the b-file).
GOAL 2(a): done (Theorem S; the statistics are the 6-window laws, support O(m)).
GOAL 2(b): NOT proved.  Theorem Q gives quadratic decoupling of head and tail inside a
parity block, but the parity-path mixture (section 4) is not controlled, so we do not get
a contracting recursion for the window laws, nor the factor 4.
IMPROVED CONSTANTS: not obtained for all n.  Theorem W gives only theta(n) <= 2 * 924 *
theta theta (worse than Sharma's 21), and max Pi = 20 occurs for many n (section 5.3), so
any bound below 21 must average, i.e. needs 2(b).  For n <= 520 (and along the chains to
1920) the exact computation gives 2.4965 <= theta(n)/(theta theta) <= 2.6947 (n >= 16).
CONJECTURE R: OPEN, with much stronger evidence: factor 4.0 per doubling up to n ~ 2000
for 3 not dividing x, and super-geometric convergence for 3 | x.  Refined form:
CONJECTURE R'.  For odd x, r(x 2^j) = Phi(x) + A(x) 4^{-j} + o(4^{-j}), with A(x) = 0
  when 3 | x (then the error is smaller than any power of 4^{-j} seen numerically), and
  |r(2m) - r(m)| / kappa(m) -> a constant (~0.65) for m = 0 mod 4.
