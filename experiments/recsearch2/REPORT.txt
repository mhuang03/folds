SECOND RECURRENCE SEARCH: NICHE FAMILIES                                (experiments/recsearch2/)
==========================================================================================

Follows experiments/recsearch/REPORT.txt (P-recursive to 30 unknowns, LLL to 64, divide-and-
conquer, convolutions, OEIS transforms, algebraic/ADE GFs of low order, mod-p BM, expression
trees).  None of those is repeated.  This search covers nonlinear shift relations, normalised
and ratio sequences, Mahler / q-difference / Bell-type functional equations, product and
2-regular structure, Hankel determinants and continued fractions, relations with the side
sequences, and mod structure.  Following the literature notes, every family that allows it
is also split by parity.

Data: a(1..38) (semi_counts.txt), c(1..41) (arith/selfdual_out.txt), J(1..33)
(jseq/out/data.txt), I(1..38) (recomputed from identity (A) and checked against data.txt),
T(N,k) for N <= 24 (rust/results/stats).  All arithmetic is exact (Fractions, python-flint)
except family 9, which is a float model fit.  The engine is recsearch/common.py: ranks mod two
61-bit primes, kernels over Q with flint, and nfit/excess as defined there.  Excess = number of
leading equations matched beyond the free-parameter count; generic = 0.  It used 1 core and
< 200 MB.

BOTTOM LINE
-----------
There are NO hits.  About 14,200 boxes were tested in total, each with at least 5 held-out
exact equations (E - K >= 5).  None has a kernel vector, i.e. no relation of any family below
holds on all data.  Every planted control was recovered: exp(x) via A(x)^2 = A(2x), the Stern
Mahler equation, Bell numbers via F' = e^z F and via the OGF substitution
B(x) = 1 + x/(1-x) B(x/(1-x)), a binomially weighted recurrence, the k! S-fraction, and
Stern / popcount / n^2 in the 2-regular test.  The near misses are small: the maximum excess
anywhere is +4, and every excess >= 2 is a structural artefact of the box (sparse columns or
tiny initial terms), not a partial fit (see NEAR MISSES).

New facts that come out of the search:
 * PROVEN (easy): a cannot satisfy any OGF Mahler equation, linear or polynomial
   P(x, F(x), F(x^2), ...) = 0, in which a(N) is solved for from the x^N coefficient.  Such an
   equation makes a(N) a bounded combination or convolution of earlier terms with constant
   coefficients, so a(N) = O(C^N), contradicting the proven super-exponential lower bound.
   (The EGF has no such obstruction, so it was searched; see M1-M4.)
 * PROVEN (standard): a(N), and any sequence that grows faster than polynomially, is not
   2-regular.  2-regular sequences are O(N^c).
 * a(k) with a(0) = 1 is NOT a Hamburger moment sequence: the Hankel determinant H_5^(0) < 0
   (signs ++++----+++-----++--).  a(k+2) is not a Stieltjes moment sequence: its S-fraction
   has negative coefficients (alpha_5 = -11.81...).  So there is no positive measure / positive
   J-fraction representation, and no total positivity of the Hankel matrix.  For a(k+1), the
   determinant H_2 = det[[1,2],[2,4]] = 0, so its S-fraction does not exist.
 * Asymptotics per parity (family 9): the conjecture's 3-parameter model
   -N ln(kappa ln N) + alpha ln N + beta fits as well as every 4-parameter alternative.
   kappa = 0.8275 (even), 0.8324 (odd), 0.8186 (3 | N), 0.8317 (N = +-1 mod 6).  The
   alternatives are: c N lnln N; a Bell saddle exp(lam(e^{cz}-1)/c); and an extra N/ln N term.
   The Bell-saddle fit parameters are not consistent across residue classes (lam = 1.32 for
   even N, 0.42 for odd N).  So 38 terms cannot separate the models, and nothing favours a
   Bell-type law.  The residual rms of 0.015-0.02 within a parity class is the mod-3
   arithmetic effect: it is 0.005 within N = +-1 (mod 6).

NEAR MISSES (honest list)
--------------------------
No partial fit extends past N = 6.  The excesses above +1 are all explained:
 * +4 in M1/M2 with m = 3 (G(x^8) columns), and +3 in M4 with G(x^2): the columns
   x^j G(x^{2^i}) are sparse (G(x^8) has only 5 non-zero coefficients up to x^38), so the
   prefix rank grows more slowly than the "generic" model assumes.  The excess measures the
   column sparsity.  The full systems are still of full column rank (no kernel).
 * +2/+3 in family 8 on A_ev / A_od: these series vanish in every other coefficient, which
   has the same effect.  All of these occur at d = 0 or dz = 0 (the smallest boxes).
 * +2 for J in 1c (r = 1, d = 0): J(1..4) = 1, 2, 2, 2.
 * +2 in M5 for A(x)/A(-x): from the first few coefficients only.
 * Family 1 with c(N): every apparent hit (71 at first) came from c(N) = 0 for N = 3 (mod 4).
   The zero pattern decouples the monomials into blocks that each have fewer equations than
   unknowns.  This is not a relation.  c is now used only through its zero-free subsequences
   c(2n) and c(4n+1).
 * Family 7: s(np) == s(n) (mod 2) holds 18/19 for a, but this is just "a(N) even for
   N >= 2".  The mod-3, -5 and -7 Gessel/Dwork counts are at chance level (a: 3/12, 2/7, 1/5;
   mod p^2: 0/12, 0/7, 0/5).  The 2-kernel of a mod 4/8/16 and the 3-kernel of a mod 8/9/16
   show no collapse beyond shuffled controls: 7/7 and 13/13 distinct elements, against
   10/13 for a mod 4 (3-kernel), which is matched by the shuffled control at 10.0.  The
   Thue-Morse control collapses to 3.  v_2(a(n)) and a(n) mod 8 are not functions of n mod 16,
   popcount(n), v_2(n), (popcount, n mod 4) or (v_2(n), n mod 8): every class with >= 2
   members conflicts.  (a - c) mod 16 has no pattern by N mod 8.
 * Hankel determinants: H_n is always divisible by about 2^n (a consequence of a(N) being
   even); after removing all prime factors < 10^5, cofactors of 7 to 77 digits remain from n ~ 7 on
   (n <= 12 shown).  This is the same "huge prime factor" picture as a(N) itself.

WHAT THIS MEANS
---------------
Together with recsearch/, the following are now ruled out on all 38 terms, in boxes of up to
about 30 unknowns:
  * linear recurrences, including P-recursive, Mahler-type and q-holonomic ones;
  * nonlinear shift relations (Somos, Riccati, cubic);
  * linear or quadratic relations for the ratio sequence a(n)/a(n-1) and many normalisations;
  * Bell-type exponential-coefficient ODEs and Moebius-substitution equations for the EGF/OGF,
    with or without the parity split;
  * linear and quadratic relations with c, J, I and T(N,k).
Every structural test (moment sequence, 2-regularity, automaticity, digit functions,
Gessel/Dwork congruences) comes out negative.  The only exact recursions known remain the
subset recursion (README) and identities (A)/(B).  Both recurse over subsets, not over earlier
terms.
