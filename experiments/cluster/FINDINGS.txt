CLUSTER / CORRELATION ANALYSIS OF a(N)  (experiments/cluster/)
==================================================================

Notation: C_m is the constraint at panel m, r_m = min(m-1, N-m), w(r) = (2r)!!/(2r+1)!!.
y(N) = log(a/a_W).  kappa(m,m') = P(C_m and C_m')/(w_m w_m') - 1 for a uniformly random permutation.
Window W_m = [m-r, m+r] with its centre included.  Arms: left arm L_m = [m-r, m-1], right arm R_m = [m+1, m+r].
Left-half windows are [1, 2m-1] and do not depend on N, so all left-left kappas are numbers that do not depend on N.

Bottom line
-----------
* Pair correlations are exactly computable. Their zero set has a clean geometric description.
  For macroscopic windows they tend to a universal function K(gamma) >= 0 of one overlap cosine.
* The second-order (pair) cluster expansion fails. Its sum is negative up to N = 24, and it then grows
  like 0.0084 N^2. That growth is impossible for y, which is at most about (1/2) N log N.
  It explains neither the drift nor the mod-3 effect.
* The Gaussian (determinant) resummation reproduces the pair limit and turns the N^2 into roughly
  N log^2 N. It overshoots y by about 0.06 N at N <= 33 and also has no mod-3 effect.
* Wallis model, exactly:
  log a_W = (N/2) log(pi N/(2e)) + (1/4) log N + (1/3) log 2 + (1/2) log pi + 3 zeta'(-1) + 1/(6N) + O(N^-2).
  This holds for both parities.
* y(N) is not linear. Best fit: y ~ 0.175 N log N - 0.55 N + const + 0.034 [3|N].
  So log a(N) ~ 0.67 N log N - 0.82 N, with an effective N log N coefficient that is still creeping up.
* Mod 3: there is a real excess delta_3 ~ 0.035 at 3 | N, decaying slowly (roughly 1/N or N^-1/2).
  It comes from the period-2 orbit {N/3, 2N/3} of the doubling map. Constraints k and 2k (N = 3k) involve
  exactly the same two prefix sets. In the full system they have the strongest long-range effective
  coupling, J ~ 0.06, about 0.26/sqrt(N). Bare pair probabilities and the Gaussian geometry miss it.
* No effect for 4, 5 or 7 | N. The 3-cycle of 7 | N shows no 3-body coupling.

1. EXACT PAIR CORRELATIONS
--------------------------
Method (pair.py exact, kappa.c floating point):
* Only the relative order of U = W_m u W_m' matters.
* Condition on the two centre times u < v. Every other element of U is before u, between u and v,
  or after v.
* Exact version: a DP over (I, J, D1, D2) gives a sum over assignments with weight I! J! K!/(n+2)!.
* Float version: Gauss-Legendre in (u, v), with a DP over (D1, D2).
* Checks: pair.py against brute force for N <= 7; kappa.c against the exact rationals (kappa_cache.txt)
  for N <= 22.

Independence. kappa = 0 exactly iff one of the following holds (checked on all 4959 pairs with N <= 33):
  (a) the windows are disjoint; or
  (b) for one of the two constraints, say a (the other is b):
      - b is not in W_a;
      - W_a meets W_b only inside one arm of b, and the overlap contains a;
      - at most one element of W_a lies outside W_b.
  Proof when no element of W_a lies outside W_b: C_b is invariant under relabelling the elements of W_a,
  because they all play the same role in C_b. Given what C_b sees, the relative order of W_a is still
  uniform. The one-outside case is exact in the data, but I have no proof for it.
Consequences:
  - Left-half m and m' >= 2m are independent.
  - Every other overlapping pair is correlated, even with no centre in the other's window.

Small closed forms:
  - Neither centre in the other's window, r = 1, overlap one element: kappa = 1/(16(2r'+3)).
  - r = 2 with the whole right arm inside the other's left arm: kappa = 61/(512(2r'+3)).
  - Other overlaps have no simple form; for example r = 3 gives (2r'+3)·kappa = 0.17399, 0.17387, 0.17378, ...
  - Adjacent left pairs, kappa(m, m+1) = -1/16, -23/768, -73/12288, 1669/131072, 4559/163840,
    169341/4194304, 95898353/2^31, ... The sign changes at m = 5, and the values then grow like about
    0.07 log m (0.102 at m = 16).
  - Doubling pairs kappa(m, 2m) for N = 51 are 0 for m <= 13 (case (b)). They are small and positive
    (at most 0.022) where 2m enters the right half.
Negative kappas occur only for small windows (a lattice effect).

Continuum limit (continuum.py, continuum_sum.py). For windows of size Theta(N), the local CLT for the
two window imbalances, given the centre times, gives
   kappa -> K(gamma),   gamma = <a_m, a_m'>/(2 sqrt(r r')),
where a_m is the +-1 role vector of the window, so gamma is the cosine between the two role vectors, and
   K(gamma) = (1/pi^2) Int Int [t(1-t)t'(1-t')]^(-1/2) (1-R^2)^(-1/2) dt dt' - 1,
   R = gamma sqrt(t(1-t')/((1-t)t'))  for t < t' (symmetric otherwise).
Properties of K:
  - K >= 0.
  - K(gamma) = (1/2 - 4/pi^2) gamma^2 + O(gamma^4). The coefficient is exact: the integral equals
    pi^2/2 - 4 after t = sin^2.
  - Values: K(0.2) = 0.00385, K(0.5) = 0.0264, K(0.8) = 0.0858, K(0.96) = 0.174; K diverges
    logarithmically as gamma -> 1.
Nested left pair with x' = lambda x (1 < lambda < 2): gamma = (2 - lambda)/sqrt(lambda).
Convergence is slow. Example (2j, 3j) with gamma = 0.408 and K = 0.0169: the exact kappa is
-0.032 (j=2), -0.0008 (j=8), +0.0054 (j=14).
The fraction of pairs with gamma != 0 tends to 1/2, the mean of gamma^2 is 0.119, and
   Sum_{m<m'} K(gamma) = C2 N^2,  C2 = 0.00840 (0.00780, 0.00810, 0.00825, 0.00833 at N = 100..800).

2. SECOND-ORDER (CLUSTER) MODEL
-------------------------------
Exact S2 = sum log(1 + kappa) (pairsum.py; pairsum.txt for N <= 33, pairsum56.txt beyond):
   N    y=log(a/aW)   S1=sum kappa   S2      y - S2
  12     0.296         -0.446       -0.458   0.754
  18     0.918         -0.443       -0.467   1.385
  24     1.854         -0.050       -0.098   1.952
  30     3.064          0.786        0.701   2.363
  33     3.678          1.397        1.285   2.393
  44      -             4.62         4.37
  54      -             9.07         8.63    (left-left 6.98, left-right 1.65)
Conclusions:
* Pairs do not explain the drift. S2 is negative for N <= 24 and then rises quadratically, heading
  for C2 N^2. Since y <= log(N!/a_W) ~ N log N/2, the pair (cluster) expansion cannot converge.
  O(N^2) pairs have O(1) correlations, and higher cumulants must cancel almost all of their sum.
  Nested left-left pairs (and their mirrors) dominate.
* Pairs do not explain the mod-3 effect. The bumps of S2 are smooth (b(S2) is about -0.02 for every N,
  bumps.txt). After subtracting S2 the mod-3 pattern is cleaner: b(y - S2) is about +0.03 at 3 | N
  and about 0 otherwise for N >= 18.
Gaussian resummation (gauss_mc.py, gauss_large.py, gauss_quenched.py):
  a_G/a_W = E_{t iid arcsine}[ det(Gamma o B(t))^(-1/2) ], where Gamma is the cosine (gamma) matrix and
  B is the Brownian-bridge correlation matrix of the centre times. Its second order is exactly K(gamma).
* Results: y_G(N) = 0.43 (N=10), 2.05 (20), 4.53 (30), 5.40 (33). This is y + 0.06 N roughly, so it
  overshoots.
* Growth: the quenched E[-1/2 log det]/(N ln^2 N) = 0.013, 0.015, 0.016, 0.018, 0.018, 0.019 for
  N = 40..1280.
* It turns the N^2 into about N log^2 N, which still eventually breaks the N log N bound.
  The local CLT cannot hold for all constraints at once.
* It has no mod-3 bump within Monte Carlo error.

3. ASYMPTOTICS
--------------
Wallis model, exact (wallis_exact.py). Write w(r) = sqrt(pi) Gamma(r+1)/(2 Gamma(r+3/2)). Then
Sum_{r<n} log w(r) = n log(sqrt(pi)/2) + log G(n+1) - log G(n+3/2) + log G(3/2) (Barnes G), and
   log a_W(N) = (N/2) log(pi N/(2e)) + (1/4) log N + gamma_W + 1/(6N) + O(N^-2),
   gamma_W = (1/3) log 2 + (1/2) log pi + 3 zeta'(-1) = 0.3071505720...
This holds for both parities. It was checked with mpmath to N = 2·10^8, and Richardson extrapolation
confirms the 1/(6N) term.

Data (asym.py, asym.txt). Fits use parity constants and [3|N]:
* y(N) is not linear: a N + b log N extrapolates badly.
* Best fits, N >= 14, rms about 0.009:
    y ~ 0.175 N ln N - 0.548 N + 1.66 (even) / 1.58 (odd) + 0.034 [3|N]
    y ~ -0.180 N + 0.049 N^1.5 + const
  Fitted on N <= 29, both predict N = 30..33 to within 0.03 in log.
* Equivalently log(N!/a)/N ~ p log N + q, with p falling slowly: about 0.366 at N ~ 12, 0.355 at
  N ~ 18 and 0.343 at N ~ 28.
Conjecture (empirical):
   log a(N) = c N log N - alpha N + (1/4) log N + const_parity + delta_3(N) [3|N] + ...
   c_eff = 0.65-0.68 (N = 15..33), alpha ~ 0.82 at c = 0.675, delta_3(N) ~ 0.7/N.
The effective coefficient c is still increasing (from 0.634 to 0.657 by local fits). The Gaussian model
also says it grows. So a(N) = N!^(c + o(1)) with c >= about 0.66. These data cannot tell c < 1 from
c = 1, which would mean the constraints become asymptotically almost implied by one another.

4. THE MOD-3 EFFECT AND OTHER MODULI
------------------------------------
Regression (moduli.py, moduli.txt). y(N) = per-parity cubic + delta_d [d | N], N >= 10:
* delta_3 = 0.035 +- 0.004, and the rms drops from 0.0215 to 0.0094.
* The fitted delta_3 varies a little with the window: 0.038 (N >= 10), 0.034 (N >= 14), 0.030 (N >= 18).
  Decaying forms fit better than a constant: 0.023 (30/N) has rms 0.0072, 0.029 sqrt(30/N) has 0.0075,
  and a constant has 0.0094.
* Most of the decay of the raw bump b(N) quoted in the task (+0.104 at N = 9 to +0.006 at N = 30) is the
  smooth convex background. b(N) for N not divisible by 3 is about -0.03. The difference
  b(3|N) - b(other) ~ 1.5 delta_3 ~ 0.05 persists to N = 30.
* N = 2 (mod 3) sits slightly above N = 1 (mod 3): +0.006 +- 0.004.
* No other modulus shows an effect:
  - delta_4 = 0.007 +- 0.0055
  - delta_5 = 0.0065 +- 0.005
  - delta_7 = -0.0025 +- 0.006
  - [6|N] and [9|N] add nothing beyond [3|N]
  - the residue-class averages of b(N) mod 4, 5, 7 and 8 are flat.

Mechanism (dpx.c, dropone.py, droptwo.py, cyclepairs.py, triples.py; exact subset DP, N <= 24).
Effective coupling inside the full system:
   J(m,m') = log[ a · a_{-m,m'} / (a_{-m} a_{-m'}) ],
where a_{-X} counts sequences with the constraints in X removed.
* For N = 3k the strongest long-range effective couplings are exactly the doubling 2-cycle (k, 2k)
  and its mirror (k+1, 2k+1):
    N        9      12     15     18     21     24
    J(k,2k)  0.080  0.073  0.076  0.057  0.062  0.052   (about 0.26/sqrt N)
  For N = 3k-1 there is one such pair, (k, 2k), which is its own mirror (0.06-0.08).
  For N = 3k+1 the analogous pairs are weak (at most 0.03).
* Sum over the near-2-cycle pairs (cyclepairs.txt):
  - 3k: 0.12-0.18
  - 3k-1: 0.08-0.10
  - 3k+1: 0.055-0.07
  The ordering 0 > 2 > 1 is the same as in the regression.
* The conditional single-site factor u_m = log[P(C_m | all others)/w] peaks at the cycle panels when
  3 | N: m = 4 for N = 9 and 12, m = 6 for N = 15, m = 8 for N = 21 (dropone.txt).
Arithmetic in prefix form (N = 3k):
   C_k:  P(2k-1) = 2 P(k-1)          C_2k:  s + P(k-1) = 2 P(2k-1)
Both constraints involve exactly the same two prefix sets [1,k-1] and [1,2k-1], in either time order.
If the counts did not change between the two times, these would force P(k-1) = s/3 and P(2k-1) = 2s/3.
In the doubling form E(2c) = 2E(c), going around the period-2 orbit {N/3, 2N/3} gives E = 4E, so E = 0.
Valid sequences have small discrepancy, so given the first constraint the second is much more likely.
That is a positive effective coupling. For N = 3k +- 1 one of the two sets is off by one panel,
the other constraint's centre, in one of the two time orders, so the loop closes only partly.
In bare (unconditioned) probabilities this coupling is invisible: kappa(k, 2k) is about 0.004 at N = 12.
It also does not appear in the Gaussian geometry. It is a renormalised effect of the constrained ensemble.
Longer cycles:
  - 7 | N has the 3-cycle {k, 2k, 4k} (and its mirror). Its exact 3-body interaction J3 is at most 0.0025
    in absolute value at N = 14 and 21, the same as for the same panels at N = 13, 15, 16, 19, 20, 22, 23, 24.
    The closing pair is not enhanced either.
  - 5 | N has a 4-cycle and shows nothing.
  So at these sizes only the 2-cycle matters, which fits the null results for d = 5 and 7.
Knock-out test (knockout.py): removing the four cycle panels and refitting is too noisy to decide
(rms 0.05), because the knocked-out geometry itself depends on N mod 3.
