PERIODS IV: THE MIXED COMPONENTS 2^a (2^s - 1) AND THE FULL PERIOD OF T(N,k)
============================================================================
(experiments/periods4/.  Notation of ../periods/PROOFS.txt (classes, face lemma, B(m),
Theorem A), ../periods2/PROOFS.txt (vertex formula, column rule, orbit/chain vertices,
Theorem C) and ../periods3/PROOFS.txt (Theorems D1, D2, non-simple vertices).
T x = 2x mod 1;  h(x) = 1/(1 - e(-x)),  g(x) = 1/(1 - e(x)) = 1 - h(x),  e(x) = exp(2 pi i x),
|h(x)| = |g(x)| = 1/(2|sin pi x|).  Throughout  k = m = a + s + 1  (vertices, c_0), a >= 1,
s >= 2,  D = 2^s - 1,  q = 2^a D.)

SUMMARY
  Question: the period of T(N,k) divides B(k) (Theorem A) and is divisible by 2^(k-1)(2^(k-1)-1)
  (Theorem C).  The remaining prime powers of B(k) live on the MIXED orders q = 2^a (2^s - 1),
  a + s = k - 1, s < k - 1, which are new at codimension k: in c_0(T(N,k)) they are carried by
  vertices only.

  1. CLASSIFICATION (section 1, proved).  A vertex carries the order q = 2^a(2^s - 1) iff it is a
     CYCLE + PENDANT CHAIN vertex: an s-periodic doubling orbit traversed in time order as a
     halving chain theta = x_{t_0} -> x_{t_1} -> ... -> x_{t_s} = theta (closed by one equality),
     plus a chain z_1, ..., z_a of successive halvings, later in time than its parent, hanging off
     a cycle point x_{t_j} through its OFF-orbit preimage.  (If z_1 hangs off theta after both
     copies of theta, the vertex is the non-simple vertex of ../periods3 8.2.)  The structures are
     parametrised by pairs (u, j), u a unit mod q (u = (q z_a)^(-1)), j the attachment index; the
     unique contributing character is explicit, and every valid vertex of the structure (u, j)
     contributes a term of the same modulus M(u, j) (closed form, Lemma 1.3).  Checked by
     exhaustion: the set of valid structured vertices equals the set of Normaliz vertices with
     q | index for every mixed q at k = 5, 6, 7, 8 (setcheck.py), and the derived lemmas below hold
     for every valid vertex for k <= 12 (classify.py, all 56962 valid vertices).
  2. THE MAIN FAMILY AND THE ROTATION LEMMA (section 2, proved).  For s >= 3 the main structure
     theta = 1/D, z_i = 1/(2^i D) (u = 1, j = 0) and its mirror are valid exactly with the time
     orders  c^s z^p c z^(a-p),  p = 0, 1, ..., min(a, 2); for a >= 2 the three terms add up to
     the first one (p = 0, the non-simple vertex), so the main family contributes EXACTLY
        Phi_main = 2 (-1)^(s+1) q^(-1) h(2^s/q) prod_{e<s} h(-2^e/q) prod_{l=1}^{a} h(-2^-l),
     of modulus 2 M(1,0);  for a = 1 the two terms nearly cancel (factor 2 sin(pi/2D)).
     ROTATION LEMMA: for s >= 4 no structure attached at 1/D or 1 - 1/D with j >= 1 is valid
     (these are the only structures whose modulus can exceed M(1,0) substantially).
  3. THEOREM F (computer-assisted, float64 with margins >= 1.26): for all s >= 2, a >= 1 with
     a + s + 1 = k <= 27, the component of exact order 2^a (2^s - 1) of c_0(T(N,k)) is nonzero.
     (s >= 4, a >= 2: one inequality "rest < 2" per (s, a) by Lemmas 2.3, 2.4; s <= 3 or a = 1:
     exact evaluation of all large structures plus a crude tail bound.)
  4. THEOREM G.  For 4 <= k <= 27:  period(c_0(T(N,k))) = period(T(N,k)) = B(k).
     (Previously certified: k <= 9.)  E.g. the period of T(N,27) is
        B(27) = 2^26 3^3 5^2 7^2 11 13 17 19 23 31 41 43 47 73 89 127 151 241 257 337 601 683
                1801 2731 8191 131071 178481 524287   (a 73-digit number).
  5. FOR ALL k: Lean (Folds/PeriodLaw2.lean, B_eq_L) proves that for k >= 9 the orders carried
     by Theorem C (2^(k-1), 2^(k-1) - 1), Theorem D1 (2^(k-2) - 1, in c_1) and the mixed orders
     with s >= 4, a >= 2 already have lcm B(k).  So "period of T(N,k) = B(k) for EVERY k >= 4" is
     reduced to ONE explicit inequality per (s, a), s >= 4, a >= 2:
        rest2(s, a) := sum_{(u,j) not main, not rotation} C(s-j+a, a) M(u,j)/M(1,0)  <  2,
     a finite trigonometric sum (no geometry left).  It holds for every k <= 27 (min margin 1.4
     at k = 9, margins >= 100 from k = 21 on; max over s of rest2 at k = 23..27: 8.3e-3, 5.8e-3,
     4.1e-3, 3.1e-3, 2.2e-3, decreasing by ~0.72 per step).  A proof of the inequality for all k is NOT given (it
     needs a uniform estimate of these products of cosecants, analogous to ../periods3 Prop. 3.1
     but with a 2-adic and a periodic part); so the period law for all k remains open, now as a
     pure inequality.
  6. CLOSED FORMS (section 4): |Phi|/M(1,0) -> 2 (s >= 4, a >= 2), = 4 sin(pi/2D) + o (a = 1),
     -> 4(a + 1) (s = 2) and -> 8a + 6 (s = 3) (for s <= 3 the rotations are valid and dominate).
  7. ONE FREE POINT (section 5): the face formula extends to the mixed structures with free
     points (mixedface.py); validated against the exact quasi-polynomial of T(N,6) (orders 12,
     14 in c_1: 12 digits).  All mixed orders new at m = 6, 7, 8 are nonzero in c_1(T(N, m+1)).


1. Which vertices carry the mixed orders
----------------------------------------
1.1 Newness.  By the face lemma, a face of codimension m has index dividing lcm_C D_C with
D_C = 2^(a_C) (tree/mark components, a_C <= e_C - 1) or 2^(a_C)(2^(s_C) - 1) (unicyclic,
a_C + s_C <= e_C - 1), sum_C e_C = m.  Let q = 2^a (2^s - 1), a >= 1, s >= 2, a + s = k - 1.
Take p a primitive prime divisor of 2^s - 1 (Zsigmondy; for s = 6 take p^2 = 9): p | 2^t - 1
iff s | t.  If q | D_F then some unicyclic C has s | s_C, so e_C >= s + 1, and some component
C' has a_C' >= a, so e_C' >= a + 1 (+ s_C' if unicyclic).  C' != C would need m >= a + s + 2 =
k + 1.  So C' = C, a_C + s_C >= a + s = k - 1 >= e_C - 1 forces m = e_C = k, a_C = a, s_C = s.
Consequences: (i) no face of codimension < k has index divisible by q ("q is new at k"), so the
order-q part of c_0(T(N,k)) is the sum over the DISTINCT vertices v with q | index(v) of the
vertex formula restricted to the characters with all w_r != 0 (../periods3 8.2); (ii) at such
a vertex any maximal independent set E of tight rows is ONE unicyclic component on all k points
with a + s = e - 1, the equality case of Lemma 5 of ../periods.

PROPOSITION 1.1 (classification).  q | index(v) iff v is a cycle + pendant chain vertex:
there are times t_0 < t_1 < ... < t_s and an s-periodic point theta with denominator D such
that x_{t_0} = x_{t_s} = theta and T(x_{t_(i+1)}) = x_{t_i} (each cycle point is a preimage of
the previous one); the other a points z_1, ..., z_a (in time order) satisfy T(z_1) = x_{t_j}
for some 0 <= j <= s - 1, z_1 != x_{t_(j+1)}, T(z_(i+1)) = z_i, and z_1 is later than t_j.
Proof.  Equality in Lemma 5 of ../periods (notation there: p, q = numbers of halving edges of
the cycle with the coefficient 2 at the start/end of the edge in a traversal, t = pendant edges
with the 2 on the far side, T = all pendant edges, e = T + cycle length):
  * min(p, q) = 0: a + s = t + p and e >= T + p + 1 (the cycle has a Q edge); equality forces
    t = T (every pendant edge is a halving whose LATER point is the far one) and a cycle made
    of p halvings and ONE equality.  Along the halvings time strictly decreases in the traversal
    direction (the 2 sits on the later point), and the later point of a halving 2y_j - y_i = c
    satisfies T(y_j) = y_i.  So the cycle is x_{t_0}, ..., x_{t_s} as stated with s = p, closed
    by the equality x_{t_s} = x_{t_0}; theta has exact period s (a smaller period s' would make
    the denominator divide 2^(s') - 1, not divisible by p).
  * min(p, q) = 1: a + s = t + max(p, q) <= t + p + q - 1 <= e - 1 with equality iff t = T and
    the cycle consists of halvings only.  The cycle point w carrying the 2 in both of its cycle
    edges is a halving of both neighbours u, u': u = 2w - c, u' = 2w - c'.  If c != c' then
    {u, u'} = {0, 1}; but a coordinate in {0,1} makes a box row tight, and choosing E through
    that mark gives a tree + mark component, impossible by 1.1.  So u = u' (the same value):
    the equality x_u = x_u' is implied, and the point configuration is that of the first case
    (this is the non-simple vertex: z_1 halving both copies of theta).
  * The pendant tree: t = T = a edges, each a halving whose far (later) point maps under T to
    the near one.  A point at depth d whose first step off the cycle is off-orbit has exact
    denominator 2^d D (the off-orbit preimage of r/D is (r + D)/(2D) or r/(2D), of exact
    denominator 2D; later halvings double the denominator); an on-orbit first step coincides
    with a cycle value.  2^a | index needs depth a, so the tree is a path z_1 .. z_a with z_1
    the off-orbit preimage.
Conversely every such configuration is a vertex of the arrangement (k independent tight rows)
with index exactly q (z_a has denominator q, all others divide q).  It is a vertex of a class
polytope iff some local cell is valid.  []

1.2 One character.  At a simple structured vertex M (the k tight rows) has |det M| = 2^a D = q
(Lemma 5: 2^t 2^min(p,q) (2^s - 1)), so the character group M^(-T)Z^k/Z^k has order q; the mode
<w, b> = <n, y> (w = M^(-T) n, M y = b) maps it onto (1/q)Z/Z because index(y) = q.  So exactly
ONE character w has mode -1/q, and all its w_r are nonzero (a w_r = 0 would make the mode an
edge character, of order not divisible by q).  The non-simple vertices are treated as in
../periods3 (nonsimple.py: each consistent sign pattern is an orthant of the basis obtained by
dropping the implied row); each basis again has |det| = q.  Hence each valid vertex contributes
ONE term (1/q) prod_r F_r(w_r) to the coefficient of e(-N/q) in c_0, F_r in {h, -h, -g}:
halving row on its side u <= 0: h(w); u > 0: -h(w); equality u > 0: -h(w); u < 0: -g(w).
By the column rule (../periods2 2.2) every column has at most one valid side assignment (for the
non-simple column of z_1 the two candidates are separated by the equality side, fixed in the
closing column), so each valid vertex has exactly one valid cell.

LEMMA 1.3 (parametrisation and moduli).  The structures (theta, j, chain) correspond
bijectively to the pairs (u, j), u in (Z/q)^*, 0 <= j < s, via  z_a = u^(-1)/q  and
theta = T^j(T^a(z_a)) (x_{t_j} = T^a z_a is the attachment value).  For the structure (u, j)
the character with mode -1/q is  w_r = -u (d z_a/d b_r) mod 1  (take n = -u e_{z_a}; the
columns of M^(-1) are d y/d b_r), i.e.
     chain rows  2z_i - z_{i-1} (z_0 := x_{t_j}):  w = -u 2^(i-1)/2^a,
     cycle rows  2x_{t_(i+1)} - x_{t_i}:           w = -u 2^((i - j) mod s)/q,
     equality    x_{t_s} - x_{t_0}:                w = +u 2^(s-j)/q,
(from x_{t_j} = (sum_i c_i 2^((i-j) mod s) - 2^(s-j) b_e)/D).  Hence every valid vertex of
(u, j) contributes a term of modulus
     M(u, j) = (1/q) prod_{l=1}^{a} |2 sin(pi u/2^l)|^-1 . prod_{e=0}^{s-1} |2 sin(pi u 2^e/q)|^-1
               . |2 sin(pi u 2^(s-j)/q)|^-1.
(Derived for simple vertices; for all valid vertices, simple and non-simple, |term| = M(u,j)
is checked by classify.py for k <= 12.)  The main structure is u = 1, j = 0 (theta = 1/D,
z_i = 1/(2^i D)) and its mirror u = -1.  Every factor of M(u,j)/M(1,0) from a chain row, or from
a cycle row with e <= a, is <= 1 (|u 2^e mod q| >= 2^e); the others can exceed 1.

LEMMA 1.4.  A structure (u, j) has at most C(s - j + a, a) time orders (t_0..t_j precede z_1;
the a chain points and the s - j cycle points t_(j+1)..t_s interleave freely), so at most that
many valid vertices.  (sum_{j<s} C(s-j+a, a) = C(s+a+1, a+1) - 1: Lean weights_sum.)

2. The main family, the rotation lemma, dominance
-------------------------------------------------
LEMMA 2.3 (main family).  Let s >= 3, a >= 1, theta = 1/D, x_{t_i} = 2^(s-i)/D (1 <= i <= s-1),
x_{t_s} = 1/D, z_i = 1/(2^i D).  The valid time orders are exactly
     t_0, t_1, ..., t_(s-1), then the chain z_1..z_a with t_s inserted after p chain points,
     p = 0, 1, ..., min(a, 2)
(p = 0 is the non-simple vertex).  Their terms are X phi_p with a common factor X and
     phi_0 = (-h_c)(-h_e),  phi_1 = h_c (-g_e),  phi_2 = (-h_c)(-g_e),
h_c = h(-2^(s-1)/q) (closing halving), h_e = h(2^s/q), g_e = g(2^s/q) (equality).  Hence for a >= 2
the family sums to X phi_0 (Lean main_family), and with the mirror
     Phi_main = 2 (-1)^(s+1) q^-1 h(2^s/q) prod_{e=0}^{s-1} h(-2^e/q) prod_{l=1}^{a} h(-2^-l),
|Phi_main| = 2 M(1,0).  For a = 1 the sum is X h_c (h_e - g_e) = -i cot(pi 2^s/q) X h_c (Lean
main_family_a1), |Phi_main| = 4 sin(pi/(2D)) M(1,0).
Proof.  Columns (sign of the later point; R(x) = (x, (1+x)/2), L(x) = (x/2, x)):
  * t_1 (b = 2^(s-1)/D = (1 + theta)/2, bit 1): chain points z < theta contribute 0 (b > (1+z)/2);
    fixed sum 0, side "outside".
  * t_i, 2 <= i <= s-1 (value x <= 2^(s-2)/D < 1/2, bit 0): +1 from theta (x in R(theta)), 0 from
    the larger cycle points (x < y/2), +1 from every earlier chain point (x in R(z)).  The tight
    row contributes 0 or -1, so no chain point may precede t_i.  As s >= 3, all chain points come
    after t_(s-1).
  * z_1 = theta/2: 0 from t_1..t_(s-1) (z_1 < y/2); tight with theta (and with t_s if later):
    side u <= 0 (both sides u <= 0 in the non-simple case).  z_i, i >= 2: 0 from everything but
    the parent; side u <= 0.
  * closing column t_s (value theta, bit 0 w.r.t. t_(s-1) = 2/D): 0 from t_1..t_(s-2); +1 from
    each of the p chain points before it (theta in R(z)); halving 0 (u <= 0) or -1 (u > 0);
    equality +1 (above) or -1 (below).  p + hc + ec = 0 has the solutions (p, hc, ec) = (0,-1,+1),
    (1, 0, -1), (2, -1, -1) only (Lean closing_column).
  * the chain points after t_s are unaffected (z_i <= theta/4 for i >= 2; z_1 is tight).
For p = 0 the non-simple pattern (z_1: both u <= 0; equality above) drops the row (z_1, t_s), so
the basis is the same graph as for p = 1, 2, and the character (Lemma 1.3) and all other factors
coincide.  The factors: halving u > 0: -h, u <= 0: h; equality above: -h(w_e), below: -g(w_e);
w_c = -2^(s-1)/q, w_e = 2^s/q; the sides of all other rows (cycle rows u > 0, chain rows u <= 0)
are the same for every p, which gives the sign (-1)^(s+1) and X.  Mirror: identical function of N.
For a = 1: h_e - g_e = 2h(w_e) - 1 = -i cot(pi w_e), and |cot(pi 2^(s-1)/D)| = tan(pi/(2D)).  []

LEMMA 2.4 (rotation lemma).  Let s >= 4, a >= 1.  No structure whose attachment value is 1/D
(u = 1 mod D) or 1 - 1/D (u = -1 mod D) with j >= 1 has a valid time order.
Proof (attachment 1/D; the other is the mirror).  x_{t_i} = 2^(j-i)/D for i <= j, so theta = 2^j/D;
x_{t_(j+1)} = b := 2^(s-1)/D (the on-orbit preimage of 1/D), and z_1 = 1/(2D).  All chain points
come after t_j.
Case j <= s - 2.  (1) A chain point placed before t_(j+1) has bit 0, i.e. is 1/(2^l D): if z_i is
the first one with bit 1, z_(i-1) = 1/(2^(i-1) D) and z_i = (1 + z_(i-1))/2 lies in R(x) for each
of the j + 1 earlier cycle points x = 2^l/D (z_i < (1+x)/2 iff z_(i-1) < x) and in R(z_l) for the
earlier chain points; the column sum is >= j + 1 + (tight >= 0) > 0.  (2) Column t_(j+1) (b, bit 1,
tight with 1/D): b is in R(2^l/D) for l = 1..j (2^s < 2^s - 1 + 2^l), and the chain points
1/(2^l D) < 1/D contribute 0; sum >= j >= 1 for both sides.  Invalid.
Case j = s - 1 (theta = b, closing column t_s).  (1) No chain point exceeding b precedes t_s: the
first such z_i has bit 1 and lies above all earlier points; it is in R(b) = R(x_{t_0}) since
z_(i-1) < b; its column is >= 1.  (2) Closing column: +1 from each of t_1..t_(s-2) (values 2^l/D,
l = 1..s-2, b in R), >= 0 from chain points (all < b, so never -1), tight halving >= 0, equality
>= -1: sum >= s - 3 >= 1.  Invalid.  []
(For s = 2, 3 the closing-column count s - 3 is <= 0, and indeed the rotations are valid and
dominant there, section 4.)

PROPOSITION 2.5 (dominance).  For s >= 4, a >= 2 let Phi be the coefficient of e(-N/q) in
c_0(T(N, a+s+1)).  Then |Phi - Phi_main| <= M(1,0) rest2(s, a), with
     rest2(s, a) = sum_{u in (Z/q)^*, j < s, (u,j) not in E} C(s-j+a, a) M(u,j)/M(1,0),
     E = {u = +-1, j = 0} u {u = +-1 mod D, j >= 1}.
So Phi != 0 whenever rest2(s, a) < 2.
Proof.  Lemmas 1.3, 1.4, 2.3, 2.4.  []

THEOREM F (computer-assisted).  For all s >= 2, a >= 1 with k = a + s + 1 <= 27 the component of
exact order 2^a (2^s - 1) of c_0(T(N,k)) is nonzero.
Proof (certify.py, certify_a1.py; logs certify_4_20.log (a >= 2), certify_21_27.log (a >= 2),
certify_a1.log).
  Path A (s >= 4, a >= 2): rest2(s, a) < 2 evaluated over all units u (numpy, float64).  Worst
     cases: (s,a) = (6,2): 1.404, (5,3): 1.279, (7,2): 1.172, (4,3): 1.166; the maximum over s of
     rest2 is, for k = 11, 12, ..., 27:  0.562, 0.411, 0.251, 0.182, 0.122, 0.086, 0.063, 0.043,
     0.031, 0.023, 0.016, 0.011, 0.0083, 0.0058, 0.0041, 0.0031, 0.0022  (ratio ~0.72 per step;
     all far above rounding).  (4,2) and (5,2) (k = 7, 8) fail the crude test and go through path B.
  Path B (s <= 3, or a = 1, or path A failed): all pairs (u, j) with C(s-j+a,a) M(u,j)/M(1,0) >
     tau are rebuilt and all their time orders checked and evaluated exactly (mixed.py); the
     rest is bounded by sum C(s-j+a,a) M(u,j).  Rotation pairs are excluded from the tail only for
     s >= 4 (Lemma 2.4); for s <= 3 they are among the exactly evaluated pairs.  tau = 1e-3 suffices
     except for a = 1, where the main family is reduced by 2 sin(pi/2D) and tau down to 1e-9 is
     used (a = 1, s = 25 needs ~1000 exact pairs; margins >= 1.26).
  For k <= 12 classify.py evaluates EVERY valid vertex exactly (no tail at all), with the same
  conclusion.  []

THEOREM G.  For 4 <= k <= 27 the constant term c_0 of T(N,k), and T(N,k) itself, have period
exactly B(k).
Proof.  The period of c_0 is the lcm of the orders of its nonzero exact-order components.  By
Theorem C (orders 2^(k-1), 2^(k-1) - 1) and Theorem F (orders 2^a(2^s - 1), a >= 1, s >= 2,
a + s = k - 1) it is divisible by lcm(2^(k-1), 2^a(2^s - 1) : a + s = k - 1, s >= 1) = B(k) (the
terms with s = 1 are powers of 2 below 2^(k-1)).  By Theorem A it divides B(k), and so does the
period of T (the lcm of the periods of the c_j, each dividing B(j') | B(k)).  []
(Independently of the a = 1 part of Theorem F: Lean B_eq_L shows for k >= 9 that C, D1 and the
path-A orders suffice.)

COROLLARY (conditional).  If rest2(s, a) < 2 for all s >= 4, a >= 2 with a + s + 1 >= 28, then
the period of T(N,k) is exactly B(k) for EVERY k >= 4 (and T(N,3) has period 6).
Proof.  Theorem G for k <= 27.  For k >= 28: Theorem C (2^(k-1), 2^(k-1) - 1 in c_0), Theorem D1
(2^(k-2) - 1 in c_1 of T(N,k), m = k - 1), Proposition 2.5 (all 2^a(2^s - 1) with s >= 4,
a >= 2 in c_0), and B(k) = lcm of these orders (Lean PeriodLaw2.B_eq_L, all k >= 9).  []
For the constant term alone the same holds with the a = 1 orders 2(2^(k-2) - 1) (Lean B_eq_L0);
those need, besides the hypothesis, the a = 1 analogue of the inequality (with main term
4 sin(pi/2D) M(1,0) instead of 2 M(1,0)), certified for s <= 25.

Why the inequality should hold (not a proof).  ratio(u,j) = M(u,j)/M(1,0) is a product of
k + 1 ratios of cosecants.  A factor stays ~1 only when u c_r = c_r (mod q) approximately, and
the units u for which MANY factors stay ~1 are u = +-1 (excluded), u = +-1 mod D with j >= 1
(rotations, excluded by Lemma 2.4), u = +-1 mod D with j = 0 and u != +-1 mod 2^a (the main orbit
with a different chain: the e < a cycle rows lose a factor ~ 2^(e-a-s) each, e.g. u = q/2 + 1:
ratio ~ pi^2 2^-(2a+s), weight C(s+a, a) <= 2^(s+a)), and u = +-3, +-5, ... (ratio ~ |u|^-(k-3)
times 2^j from the equality row).  The data show exactly these as the largest terms of rest2
(r2top.py: k = 21, (13,7): u = -+(q/2 - 1)... j = 0 terms 5.7e-3 each, then u = +-3), and every one
of these families gives a contribution decaying geometrically in k; what is missing is the
uniform bound on the union of all the other units (as in ../periods3 Prop. 3.1, but now with the
2-adic part u mod 2^a and the periodic part u mod D interacting through the e < a rows).


3. The prime powers and the reduction to B(k) (Lean)
----------------------------------------------------
For a prime p, v_p(B(k)) = max_{s <= k-1} v_p(2^s - 1) is attained exactly at the multiples s of
s_0 = ord_p(2) p^J <= k - 1 (../periods3 8.1).  Theorem C carries p^v when s_0 | k - 1, Theorem D1
when s_0 | k - 2, Proposition 2.5 whenever some multiple s of s_0 has 4 <= s <= k - 3.  If none
of these happens then every multiple of s_0 in [1, k-1] is <= 3 or >= k - 2, which for k >= 9
is impossible (2^6 - 1 = 63 contains 3 and 7, and 6 <= k - 3).  This is the content of
     PeriodLaw2.B_eq_L  (k >= 9):  B(k) = lcm(2^(k-1), 2^(k-1) - 1, 2^(k-2) - 1,
                                              2^(k-1-s)(2^s - 1) : 4 <= s <= k - 3),
     PeriodLaw2.B_eq_L0 (k >= 9):  the same with 2(2^(k-2) - 1) in place of 2^(k-2) - 1,
proved for all k in Lean (no sorry, no native_decide), together with the bookkeeping identities
hg_sum (h + g = 1), main_family, main_family_a1, closing_column (Lemma 2.3) and weights_sum
(Lemma 1.4).


4. Closed forms and the small cycles
------------------------------------
For s >= 4, a >= 2 the component is Phi_main (Lemma 2.3, explicit) plus a relative error
rest2 (<= 0.016 for k >= 21):  |Phi|/M(1,0) = 2.000000 (k = 11: 1.999997..2.000000).  With
M(1,0) = (1/q) prod_{l=1}^{a} (2 sin(pi/2^l))^-1 prod_{e=0}^{s} (2 sin(pi 2^e/q))^-1
           ~ G_a q^s / ((2 pi)^(s+1) 2^(s(s+1)/2)),  G_a = prod_{l<=a} (2 sin(pi/2^l))^-1,
the component grows like 2^(s(s-1)/2 + a s) (e.g. k = 11, (4,6): |Phi| = 1.04e7).
For a = 1, s >= 4: |Phi| = 4 sin(pi/(2D)) M(1,0) (1 + o(1)) (the two main vertices nearly cancel:
the factor is cot(pi 2^s/q), 2^s/q = 1/2 + 1/(2D)); e.g. s = 24: 3.745e-7 M(1,0); the crude bound on
all other structures is below 1/1.26 of it for s <= 25 (certify_a1.log).  This is the vertex-level counterpart of the
near-cancellation 126: 0.233 + 9.024i against 0.227 - 9.095i at k = 8 (../periods3 8.3).
For s = 2, 3 the rotation lemma fails (closing column count s - 3 <= 0): the rotation
(u = 1, j = s - 1: theta = 2^(s-1)/D, chain at 1/D) is valid with a + 1 time orders per mirror,
all of the same phase, and its modulus is sin(pi 2^s/q)/sin(pi 2/q) ~ 2^(s-1) times M(1,0):
     s = 2:  |Phi|/M(1,0) = 4(a + 1) + o(1)      (a = 5..19: 23.99, 28.00, 32.00, ..., 80.00),
     s = 3:  |Phi|/M(1,0) = 8a + 6 + o(1)        (a = 5..19: 45.93, 53.98, 61.99, ..., 150.0)
(s = 2: the rotation has ratio -> 2 and 2(a+1) time orders (both mirrors), all in phase;
s = 3: ratio -> 4, 2(a+1) time orders, minus the main family 2 M(1,0) of opposite phase:
8(a+1) - 2).  These orders
(3.2^(k-3), 7.2^(k-4)) are not needed for the period of T (Lean B_eq_L), but they are part of
the full Fourier structure of c_0.
Exact totals for k <= 12 (all vertices): classify.log.


5. One free point
-----------------
For c_1(T(N, m+1)) the mixed orders q = 2^a(2^s - 1), a + s = m - 1, are carried by the
codimension-m faces: a cycle + pendant chain structure on m of the points and one free point z
(the same newness argument: the free point carries no tight row).  The face formula
(../periods2 section 1) becomes: for every structure, every time order of the m structured
points, every gap and every z-interval between the breakpoints (v, v/2, (1+v)/2, 2v, 2v-1), the
valid cell (unique) is determined and its term (1/q) prod F_r(w_r) (character of the structured
rows, Lemma 1.3) is weighted by the length of the interval (mixedface.py).  The free point can
change the sides of the structured columns, so the weights are signed lengths per side pattern.
