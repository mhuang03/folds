T(N,5) and T(N,6) exactly: Ehrhart quasi-polynomials via Normaliz
=================================================================

T(N,k) = number of valid k-step prefixes (k distinct panels of [1..N], every step
semi-symmetric).  See ../prefix/FINDINGS.txt for the polytope mechanism.

RESULT (exact, derived; no fitting)
  T(N,5): degree 5, period 1680 = 16*3*5*7 (exactly; attained only in the constant term).
  T(N,6): degree 6, period 104160 = 32*3*5*7*31 (exactly; attained only in the constant term).
  T(N,7): period exactly 624960 = 64*9*5*7*31 (no full table).
  General: period(T(N,k)) = B(k) = lcm(2^{k-1}, 2^a (2^s - 1) : a + s = k - 1), k = 2..7 except
  k = 3 (6 = B(3)/2).  The coefficient of N^{k-m} has period B(m), independent of k.
  Both quasi-polynomials hold for ALL N >= 0, with no exceptional small N.
  As a byproduct, the fitted T(N,4) of ../prefix (period 168) is now derived exactly.


1. Method
---------
ehrhart.py.  A valid sequence x_1..x_k falls into exactly one class (pi, sigma): pi is its order
type, and sigma_ij (i < j) is +1/-1/0 as x_j lies in the right arm, the left arm, or neither
arm of x_i (the same classification as ../prefix/pieces.c).  Validity means each column of
sigma sums to 0.  For a fixed class, every condition is a linear inequality with integer
coefficients in (x_1..x_k, N):
   1 <= x_i <= N;   x_v >= x_u + 1 along pi;
   sigma=+1: 2x_j <= N + x_i;    sigma=-1: 2x_j >= x_i + 1;
   sigma=0:  2x_j >= N + x_i + 1 (x_j > x_i),   2x_j <= x_i (x_j < x_i).
So the class is the set of lattice points of one rational polyhedron Q in R^{k+1}.  Graded by
N, Normaliz (PyNormaliz 2.24, `pip install pynormaliz`; inhomogeneous input) returns its
Hilbert series sum_N #P(N) t^N = t^s num(t) / prod(1 - t^d) EXACTLY.  The classes are generated
step by step (rank of the new point, then a balanced choice of earlier points in its arms),
and classes with zero Hilbert series are pruned.  The classes partition the valid sequences, so
   sum_N T(N,k) t^N = sum over classes of these rational functions,
with no assumption: the window rule (sigma determined by pi) is not used, and is confirmed
again ("0 order types with >1 class").
   k        2  3  4   5    6
   classes  2  6  24  116  660     (the same as contpatterns.py; 384 and 3712 candidates)
   time: k = 5 in 10 s, k = 6 in about 1 min (1 core).

combine.py / qpoly.py.  For every class, deg(num) + s < sum(d) (checked), so the coefficient of
t^N is a quasi-polynomial of period lcm(d) for every N >= 0 (not only for large N).  Each class's
quasi-polynomial is obtained by exact interpolation from N = r + m*lcm(d), m = 0..k.  These are
summed, and the sum is checked against the series expansion (k = 5: N = 0..11760, i.e. 7 full
periods; k = 6: N = 0..300).  Each coefficient c_j(N) of T = sum_j c_j(N) N^j is decomposed into
exact-order Fourier components g_d(N mod d), using exact rationals and Moebius inversion over the
averages.
compact.py: the tables below (T5_compact.txt, T6_compact.txt), re-checked against the series.
Full tables: qp5.txt (all 1680 residue polynomials), qp5_c*.txt, qp6_c1..4.txt; c_0 of
T(N,6) only in T6_compact.txt (qp6_c0.txt, 3 MB, is git-ignored; it is regenerated by qpoly.py 6).

5. Structure of the periods
---------------------------
The period of T(N,k) is 2, 6, 168, 1680, 104160, 624960 for k = 2..7.  Coefficient by coefficient the
periods form a staircase that does not depend on k:

   period of c_{k-m}(N), the coefficient of N^{k-m}:
        m = 0, 1:  1 (constant)
        m = 2:     2
        m = 3:     12          (6 for k = 3: the 4-part cancels in the constant term of T(N,3))
        m = 4:     168
        m = 5:     1680
        m = 6:     104160
        m = 7:     624960      (k = 7, section 4b)
   verified exactly for every coefficient of T(N,3)..T(N,6), and by the difference test of
   section 4b for T(N,7).

So the period of T(N,k) is B(k), with
   B(m) = lcm( 2^{m-1}, 2^a (2^s - 1) for a + s = m - 1, s >= 1 ):
   B(2) = lcm(2, 1) = 2,  B(3) = lcm(4, 3) = 12,  B(4) = lcm(8, 2*3, 7) = 168,
   B(5) = lcm(16, 4*3, 2*7, 15) = 1680,  B(6) = lcm(32, 8*3, 4*7, 2*15, 31) = 104160.
B(k) is also exactly the lcm of the class periods from Normaliz (k <= 6), and equals the
vertex-denominator bound of ../prefix/verts.py (k <= 5).  It is attained for k = 2, 4, 5, 6,
and halved for k = 3.  Better still, the exact-order Fourier components of c_{k-m} are exactly the divisors of the numbers
2^{m-1} and 2^a (2^s - 1), a + s = m - 1, s >= 2:
   m = 5: divisors of 16, 12, 14, 15  = {1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,12,14,15,16}           (T5 c0, T6 c1)
   m = 6: divisors of 32, 24, 28, 30, 31 = {1,...,8,10,12,14,15,16,24,28,30,31,32}  (T6 c0)
   m = 4: divisors of 8, 6, 7         = {1,2,3,4,6,7,8}                          (T4 c0, T5 c1, T6 c2)
In particular, no component has an odd part that is not a divisor of a single 2^s - 1
(no 9, 21, 35, 105), and no 48 = 16*3.  So each coefficient is a SUM of functions of N mod
2^a (2^s - 1), one for each split a + s = m - 1, never a function of a larger modulus.

Mechanism.  A face of codimension m of P_pi(N) is cut out by m tight relations among
2x_j = x_i, 2x_j = x_i + N, x_u = x_v (continuum) and x = 0, N.  Along a path, each halving
relation multiplies by 1/2 or 2.
  * A chain of a halvings hanging from a box face (x = 0 or N) gives denominator 2^a.
  * A cycle whose net exponent is s gives x = 2^{-s} x + const, i.e. denominator 2^s - 1 (only
    "minus": the multipliers are positive powers of 2, so 2^s + 1 never occurs; indeed no
    9 = 2^3 + 1 and no 17).
  * Combined, the coordinates of the vertex (and the affine hull) of such a face have
    denominators 2^a (2^s - 1), with a + s <= m - 1.  One relation is used up to close the
    cycle (an equality x_u = x_v, or a reversed halving).
By McMullen's theorem, the period of the coefficient of N^{k-m} divides the lcm of the
denominators of the codimension-m faces, which gives the staircase.  For the constant term this
is the vertex bound B(k).  The data show that the bound is attained for k >= 4.
These are the doubling-map orbits of the README's cyclic form E(2c) = 2E(c): 2^s - 1 is the
number of periodic points of x -> 2x mod 1 of period dividing s, and 2^a is the number of
preimages along a chain.  The same primes appear in the denominators of the leading
coefficients: 7 at k = 4; 5, 7 at k = 5; 7, 31 at k = 6.
k = 7 confirms this (section 4b): the period is B(7) = lcm(64, 16*3, 8*7, 4*15, 2*31, 63) =
624960 = 2^6 3^2 5 7 31, with 9 appearing for the first time (from 63 = 2^6 - 1).
